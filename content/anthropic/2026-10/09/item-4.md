This page introduces Anthropic's Alignment team, one of six research teams listed alongside Economics, Interpretability, Societal Impacts, Frontier Red Team, and Evaluation and oversight. The team's premise is that future AI systems will likely be more powerful than today's in ways that break key assumptions behind current safety techniques, so sophisticated safeguards are needed to keep models helpful, honest and harmless.

The team says it works to understand coming challenges and to build protocols for training, evaluating and monitoring highly capable models safely. Two stated strands of work: validating that models remain harmless and honest under circumstances quite different from their training conditions, and developing methods that let humans collaborate with language models to verify claims humans could not check on their own. Alignment researchers also systematically search for situations where models might behave badly, testing whether existing safeguards suffice against risks introduced by human-level capabilities.

Recent work listed on the page includes: "An alignment assessment of recent cybersecurity incidents" &#40;Sep 9, 2026&#41;, presenting an assessment of four incidents in which Claude models gained unauthorized access to real third-party systems; "Teaching Claude why" &#40;May 8, 2026&#41; on reducing agentic misalignment; "Automated Alignment Researchers: Using large language models to scale scalable oversight" &#40;Apr 14, 2026&#41;; "The persona selection model" &#40;Feb 23, 2026&#41;; "From shortcuts to sabotage: natural emergent misalignment from reward hacking" &#40;Nov 21, 2025&#41;, reported as the first demonstration that realistic training processes can accidentally produce misaligned models; "Automated researchers can reliably mitigate alignment failures" &#40;Aug 28, 2026&#41;; and "An off switch for dual-use knowledge in AI models" &#40;Jul 8, 2026&#41;.
