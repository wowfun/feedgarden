This page introduces Anthropic's Economics team, one of six listed research teams. The team studies how AI is reshaping the economy, including work, productivity and economic opportunity, and says it aims to build the empirical foundation for understanding AI's economic impact through data collection and analysis.

Its flagship effort is the Anthropic Economic Index, which tracks how AI tools are actually used around the world and across sectors, and the team says the goal is to move beyond speculation to measure adoption patterns as they unfold. Alongside the index reports, the team produces research on the implications of AI usage and diffusion—as tracked in the index—for workers, firms and the broader economy. The stated motivation is that economic transitions create both opportunity and disruption, and that the speed of AI development raises the stakes, so workers, employers and policymakers need reliable data.

Recent work listed on the page includes: a new model of how AI may affect economic growth, jobs and wages by 2030, which lets users explore scenarios, submit predictions and compare answers with more than 10,000 Americans; the Anthropic Economic Index report "Cadences" &#40;Jun 26, 2026&#41;, which samples hourly for the first time to ask when people come to Claude, what they produce and how they perceive AI's impact on their work; "Learning curves" &#40;Mar 24, 2026&#41;, the fifth Economic Index report, studying Claude usage in February 2026 and building on the economic primitives framework; "What 81,000 people told us about the economics of AI" &#40;Apr 22, 2026&#41;, a survey connecting users' economic concerns with quantified Claude traffic, and the associated survey announcement; and "Labor market impacts of AI: A new measure and early evidence" &#40;Mar 5, 2026&#41;. Other publications cover what work robots can do &#40;Sep 30, 2026&#41;, Project Swap on agents trading on our behalf &#40;Sep 24, 2026&#41;, evidence on worker retraining programs &#40;Aug 12, 2026&#41;, and country-level findings for Canada &#40;Jul 14, 2026&#41; and Australia &#40;Mar 31, 2026&#41;, along with agentic coding and coding agents in the social sciences.
