This page introduces Anthropic's Interpretability team, one of six listed research teams. Its stated mission is to discover and understand how large language models work internally, as a foundation for AI safety and for positive outcomes.

The team argues it is very challenging to reason about the safety of neural networks without understanding them, and that its goal is to explain models' behaviors in detail and then use that understanding to address problems ranging from bias to misuse to autonomous harmful behavior. The page notes a deliberately multidisciplinary approach: some researchers have deep machine learning backgrounds — one is described as having started mechanistic interpretability, another as having worked on the well-known scaling laws paper — while others joined after careers in astronomy, physics, mathematics, biology and data visualization.

Recent research listed includes: "A global workspace in language models" &#40;Jul 6, 2026&#41;, described as revealing an emergent mental workspace in Claude that holds internal thoughts not appearing in the model's output; "Natural Language Autoencoders: Turning Claude's thoughts into text" &#40;May 7, 2026&#41;, which trains Claude to translate its numerical internal representations into human-readable text; "Emotion concepts and their function in a large language model" &#40;Apr 2, 2026&#41;, investigating what underlies aparent emotional behavior; "A 'diff' tool for AI: Finding behavioral differences in new models" &#40;Mar 13, 2026&#41;; "The assistant axis: situating and stabilizing the character of large language models" &#40;Jan 19, 2026&#41;, on the character most modern models inhabit with users; "Signs of introspection in large language models" &#40;Oct 29, 2025&#41;, reporting evidence of a limited but functional ability to introspect; "Persona vectors: Monitoring and controlling character traits in language models" &#40;Aug 1, 2025&#41;; and open-sourced circuit tracing tools &#40;May 29, 2025&#41;.
