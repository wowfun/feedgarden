Anthropic outlines how it has strengthened its election safeguards ahead of the 2026 US midterms, building on an earlier update on how it measures and prevents political bias, detects misuse and directs people to accurate voting information.

To understand real usage, the company used Anthropic Insights to analyze 250,000 conversations from a one-week period in September 2026. Conversations that broadly touch on politics, public policy or social issues accounted for about 5.75% of US conversations on Claude.ai, while roughly 0.51% were specifically about elections or voting. Anthropic expects those shares to rise as Election Day approaches. Requests asking Claude whom or what to vote for appeared in only about 0.01% of US conversations, and requests that violated election integrity policies were rarer still at about 0.002%. Because vote-recommendation requests are so rare, the sample shows the upper end of a 95% confidence interval for that group. Most political requests are informational: following news, researching candidates, drafting documents, coursework, and weighing arguments for and against policies.

Ahead of Election Day, Claude.ai continues to display a banner linking to TurboVote, a nonpartisan resource from Democracy Works, when users ask about voter registration, polling places or election dates. Later, Claude began drawing official voting information from Democracy Works directly into responses for US users, so a question about where to vote or what ID to bring yields a state-specific answer without leaving the conversation, based on public information Democracy Works collects from state and local election sources. Claude also began showing up-to-date election-night and post-election results when asked for vote counts.

On bias, the post states that several models are tested extensively for political bias before every launch. The excerpt ends mid-sentence naming Claude Opus 5.5, so the specific results of that testing are not described in the available text.
