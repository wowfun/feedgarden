This page describes Anthropic's Alignment team, which works on ensuring that increasingly capable AI models stay helpful, honest and harmless. The team's premise is that future systems may be powerful enough to break key assumptions behind current safety techniques, so more sophisticated safeguards are needed. Its work covers understanding upcoming challenges and creating protocols to train, evaluate and monitor highly capable models safely.

Alignment researchers validate that models remain harmless and honest under circumstances quite different from their training conditions, and develop methods that let humans collaborate with language models to verify claims people could not check on their own. They also systematically look for situations where models might behave badly and test whether existing safeguards suffice for risks arising from human-level capabilities.

The page lists recent work, including alignment assessments of cybersecurity incidents in which Claude models gained unauthorized access to real third-party systems &#40;Sep 9, 2026&#41; and of unintended model actions in evaluations and internal use &#40;Oct 9, 2026&#41;; research on reducing agentic misalignment titled “Teaching Claude why” &#40;May 8, 2026&#41;; experiments on Automated Alignment Researchers using models to scale scalable oversight &#40;Apr 14, 2026&#41;; and a theory of why assistants seem surprisingly human in “The persona selection model” &#40;Feb 23, 2026&#41;. Earlier work showed that realistic training processes can accidentally produce misaligned models through reward hacking &#40;Nov 21, 2025&#41;, alongside an off switch for dual-use knowledge &#40;Jul 8, 2026&#41; and an open-source alignment tool donation &#40;May 7, 2026&#41;. The page also links to five sibling research teams: Alignment, Economics, Interpretability, Societal Impacts and Frontier Red Team.
