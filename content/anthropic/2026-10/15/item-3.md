This page describes Anthropic's Economics team, which studies how AI is reshaping the economy, including work, productivity and economic opportunity. The team collects and analyzes data to track AI's real-world economic effects and publishes research aimed at helping policymakers, businesses and the public understand and prepare for coming changes.

Its stated goal is to build an empirical foundation for understanding AI's economic impact. The flagship Anthropic Economic Index tracks how AI tools are actually used around the world and across sectors, aiming to measure adoption patterns as they unfold rather than rely on speculation. Alongside the index reports, the team produces research on the implications of AI usage and diffusion, as tracked in the index, for workers, firms and the broader economy. The team argues that economic transitions create both opportunity and disruption, and that the speed of AI development makes the stakes unusually high, so reliable data is needed to inform decisions by workers, employers and policymakers.

Recent projects listed include a new model of how AI may affect economic growth, jobs and wages through 2030, which readers can use to explore scenarios and compare answers with more than 10,000 Americans &#40;June 2026&#41;; the sixth Economic Index report, “Cadences,” which samples hourly for the first time to ask when people come to Claude, what they produce and how they perceive AI's impact on their work &#40;June 26, 2026&#41;; the fifth report, “Learning curves,” on February 2026 usage &#40;March 24, 2026&#41;; a survey study of 81,000 Claude users connecting economic concerns with quantified traffic data &#40;April 22, 2026&#41;; and a framework for measuring AI's labor market impacts tested against early data &#40;March 5, 2026&#41;. Publication titles also include work on what robots can do &#40;Sep 30, 2026&#41;, Project Swap on agents trading for people &#40;Sep 24, 2026&#41;, evidence on worker retraining programs &#40;Aug 12, 2026&#41;, and country-level Economic Index findings for Canada &#40;Jul 14, 2026&#41; and Australia &#40;Mar 31, 2026&#41;.
