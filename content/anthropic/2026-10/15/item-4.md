This page describes Anthropic's Frontier Red Team, which stress-tests AI systems to understand the full extent of their current capabilities and anticipate what comes next. The team says it provides evidence-based analysis of AI's implications for cybersecurity, national security and autonomous systems.

The page highlights a robotics experiment, Project Fetch: Phase two, which tests whether Claude can help Anthropic employees perform sophisticated and amusing robotics tasks. Broader publication titles indicate the team's range: a claim that AI models may be capable of self-replicating without access to a datacenter &#40;Oct 12, 2026&#41;; an opt-in vulnerability-finding service for open-source software &#40;Oct 8, 2026&#41;; work on GLM-5.3 and the spread of advanced cyber capabilities &#40;Sep 29, 2026&#41;; measurement of tactical intelligence targeting and conventional weapons capabilities of AI models &#40;Sep 10, 2026&#41;; patterns and problems in emerging multiagent systems &#40;Aug 13, 2026&#41;; discovering cryptographic weaknesses with Claude &#40;Jul 28, 2026&#41;; whether AI can control a drone, in Project Pilot &#40;Jul 24, 2026&#41;; Claude playing robotics &#40;Jul 9, 2026&#41;; and measuring the impact of large language models on N-day exploits &#40;Jun 8, 2026&#41;.

The team sits alongside four sibling research groups listed on the page: Alignment, Economics, Interpretability and Societal Impacts. The page also links to a research careers section for open roles.
