This page describes Anthropic's Interpretability team, whose mission is to discover and understand how large language models work internally, as a foundation for AI safety and positive outcomes. The team argues that reasoning about the safety of neural networks is very difficult without understanding them, so its goal is to explain model behavior in detail and then use that understanding to address problems ranging from bias to misuse to autonomous harmful behavior.

The team takes a multidisciplinary approach. The page notes that some researchers have deep machine learning backgrounds, including a member often described as having started mechanistic interpretability and another who worked on the scaling laws paper, while others joined after careers in astronomy, physics, mathematics, biology and data visualization.

Recent publications include research revealing an emergent mental workspace in Claude that holds internal thoughts not appearing in the model's output &#40;Jul 6, 2026&#41;; Natural Language Autoencoders, which train Claude to translate its internal representations into human-readable text, framed as models that talk in words but think in numbers &#40;May 7, 2026&#41;; a study of emotion concepts and their function in a large language model &#40;Apr 2, 2026&#41;; and “The assistant axis: situating and stabilizing the character of large language models” &#40;Jan 19, 2026&#41;. Earlier work includes signs of a limited but functional ability for models to introspect and report on their own internal states &#40;Oct 29, 2025&#41;, persona vectors for monitoring and controlling character traits &#40;Aug 1, 2025&#41;, open-sourced circuit tracing tools &#40;May 29, 2025&#41;, tracing the thoughts of a large language model &#40;Mar 27, 2025&#41;, and auditing language models for hidden objectives &#40;Mar 13, 2025&#41;. A “diff” tool for finding behavioral differences in new models appeared Mar 13, 2026. The page also links to a research careers section.
