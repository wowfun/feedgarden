Anthropic researchers surveyed a nationally representative sample of US adults and the company's own staff in August 2026, aiming to separate disagreement about how fast AI capabilities will improve from disagreement about how quickly those capabilities will be adopted.

The two groups largely agree about what AI can do today. Asked about eight tasks, from writing routine emails to making a Nobel-level scientific discovery, they rank them in nearly the same order of difficulty, and around a quarter of both groups think AI can already design, conduct and publish scientific studies. They diverge on the future: 39% of US adults and 91% of Anthropic staff expect AI to be able to make a Nobel-level discovery by 2030.

Staff also expect larger productivity gains on tasks AI suits. Among US adults who use AI at work, 26% say a task now takes a quarter of the time or less, compared with 59% of staff. On average, staff expect AI to be able to do 75% of their current work by 2030, versus 28% for US workers overall; over half of staff expect at least 80%, against 5% of US workers.

On the labor market the two groups reason similarly. People who expect AI to do more of their own work forecast higher unemployment, and at the same expected level of automation both groups forecast roughly the same unemployment rate. The survey also covered how autonomous AI will be, how much time it saves, and how hard it would be for a displaced worker to find a job in a different occupation.
