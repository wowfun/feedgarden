Multimodal large language models &#40;MLLMs&#41; perform poorly on spatial, embodied, physical and temporal reasoning. The authors hypothesize these failures share a deficit in visual transition reasoning, and test whether that capability can act as a shared training primitive that different models learn from different supervision sources and reuse across tasks. Existing benchmarks document such deficits separately but do not support controlled comparisons across scenes, actions and reasoning operations.

They introduce WOVEN, a training source and benchmark that organizes transition supervision by scene, action and reasoning type, built from diverse realistic rollouts generated by video-pretrained generative models: 36,076 examples spanning 20 scene types, 5 action types and 8 reasoning types. Evaluating 38 frontier MLLMs, including GPT-5.4 and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B, shows a substantial, systematic deficit: even the strongest models fall far below humans, and failures recur across model families and persist as scale grows.

Training MLLMs at multiple scales on WOVEN yields a capability that transfers broadly. Subsets of only about 2,000 items each collectively improve 22 of 26 external benchmarks by up to 27.3 percentage points, and WOVEN data can replace 30-50% of a task's own training data at comparable accuracy. Controlled comparisons produce a recipe for visual world modeling, validated prospectively on held-out benchmarks: select supervision by the reasoning operation it teaches rather than by the actions, scenes or domains it shows, and prefer larger changes to the visual state for robustness.
