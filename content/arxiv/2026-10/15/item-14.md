Today's agentic systems are typically designed, built and deployed by single developers who have full control over all participating agents. That simplifies development but limits the potential scale of agentic systems. The authors study multi-developer agentic systems &#40;MDAs&#41;, in which developers collaboratively build agentic systems to push frontier capability beyond what any individual could reach alone.

They construct empirical and theoretical models of developers to compare MDAs against single-developer agentic systems &#40;SDAs&#41;. On GAIA, a general assistant benchmark, a population of specialized developers whose individual agents do not exceed 35% accuracy reaches 70% when their agents are combined in an MDA. That MDA performs 12 percentage points above the median general-purpose SDA built with several times each specialist's development budget.

The theoretical models show how MDAs can incentivize specialization and encourage more efficient use of aggregated developer resources. The authors frame the results as a preliminary case for MDAs as a new scaling axis, enabling developers with diverse capabilities and resources to advance agentic AI collaboratively.
