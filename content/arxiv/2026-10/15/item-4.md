Retrieval-augmented generation &#40;RAG&#41; has become close to a cornerstone of open-domain question answering, with answer quality depending heavily on how effective retrieval is. Over the past two years many RAG architectures have reported improvements from various design innovations. South Asian cultural question answering is a specific challenge because the region contains a very large number of closely interrelated languages and traditions, and its deeply intertwined historical narratives increase the need for culturally grounded, context-aware interpretation. Despite architectural advances, region-specific questions in native languages remain difficult.

The study aims to extract high-performing factors that can guide development of optimized hybrid RAG systems. It asks whether integrating RAG changes performance differently for culturally grounded versus non-cultural questions in a South Asian multilingual setting, and whether RAG yields a higher overall percentage of correct answers than contexted answering. It also compares RAG against non-contexted and contexted answering pipelines, and examines whether adding RAG benefits popular or non-popular culturally grounded questions more.

The stated contribution is both evaluative and forward-looking: the authors position the work as assessing current methods while carving a path toward more accurate and efficient question-answering pipelines. No concrete benchmark results, dataset sizes or model names are reported in the available text.
