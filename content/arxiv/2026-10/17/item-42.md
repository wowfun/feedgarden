Scientific inquiry proceeds through repeated cycles of exploration, hypothesis generation and reasoning over observations to uncover relationships in complex systems. LLMs show strength in several of these components — inductive reasoning, hypothesis generation and data analysis — but prior work evaluates them in isolation, leaving open how models integrate them across a full inquiry cycle.

The authors introduce a framework for evaluating inquiry capabilities in a unified, interactive setting. They extend the Countdown task into a mathematical environment in which an agent probes the system by manipulating variables, collecting observations and inferring relationships between them. This makes it possible to analyze exploration strategies, causal discovery and hypothesis refinement during inquiry.

Across seven LLMs and multiple prompting strategies, the experiments reveal limited inherent inquiry capabilities and low success rates in discovering the underlying expressions. Structured guidance inspired by educational scaffolding significantly improves performance.

The authors conclude that current LLMs have limitations in strategic exploration and iterative hypothesis refinement, and that simple guidance strategies developed for human learners in inquiry learning can support more effective inquiry behavior. The work is by Marta Knežević, Seyed Parsa Neshaei, Jibril Frej, Vinitra Swamy and Tanja Käser.
