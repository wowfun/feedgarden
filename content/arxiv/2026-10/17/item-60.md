Researchers built a general-purpose AI tutor for computing courses that accepts student questions in a foreign language &#40;currently Mandarin&#41; and answers bilingually, aiming to reduce language barriers faced by non-native English speaker &#40;NNES&#41; students as international enrollment grows. The system also generates glossaries that explain technical terms to support comprehension.

The tutor uses a specialized retrieval-augmented generation pipeline with a new question-extraction mechanism, letting it draw answers from a diverse set of course materials rather than a single source. The authors evaluated it on 91 Piazza posts. It answered 83.52% of the posts correctly and helpfully, produced high-quality translations for 76.92% of the questions, and generated technical-term glossaries for 64.47% of them.

The authors say the results underscore the potential of multi-source RAG pipelines for AI tutors and for supporting non-native speakers, a group that is hard to serve with the traditional supporting staff available in a computing course. The reported figures come from that 91-post evaluation; the abstract does not describe a comparison with human tutors or other tutor systems.
