## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters highlight a visual diff harness the author supplied as proof that the port replicates the original game pixel for pixel. One reader frames this as a demonstration of a new collective capability: getting computers to do work we could describe but would probably never take the time to do ourselves; another replies that this kind of visual regression testing predates LLMs, citing Dolphin's visual CI system as prior inspiration.

The thread also carries skepticism. One commenter asks whether crowds of developers will now port open-source software to Rust with LLMs and pass it off as their own, adding that they see no need since the C++ code was already largely bug-free; a reply corrects this, noting Quake was written in C, not C++, and that Doom 3 around 2004 was id's first C++ game. The author responds that the project took about four months as a hobby, a mix of work and "waiting for the next model," and says they find the LLM discourse strangely sad: the tools are annoying and err daily yet have been among the most rewarding things they have worked with. Others call the browser experience impressive and note that they have been online since the late 1990s or early 2000s. One commenter says alternate projects such as CSS and ASCII Quake feel like a bigger challenge.
