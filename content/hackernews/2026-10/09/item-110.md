## Article

The Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize to South African judge Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, it announced Friday in Oslo. Committee chair Jorgen Watne Frydnes said Pillay had been "instrumental in ensuring that war crimes, crimes against humanity, and genocide are prosecuted."

Pillay headed the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry, which last year found that genocide was occurring in Gaza; the committee stressed the COI does not speak for the UN. She is currently a judge at the International Court of Justice in a case involving accusations of genocide against Myanmar, and earlier led the Rwanda genocide tribunal. She said her team had shared "thousands of pieces of information" with International Criminal Court prosecutors.

The committee said it was honoring Pillay as "the system of international law is under tremendous pressure and its institutions are under attack," describing international law as "an absolute necessity" rather than a supplement to peace and security, and citing her "firm moral compass." The committee denied taking a position on whether Israel is committing genocide in Gaza, saying that question is for courts to decide. Israel condemned the award, alleging it legitimized "anti-Israel hatred."

The prize includes a gold medal, a diploma and &#36;1.2 million, presented in Oslo on December 10. The literature prize went to Canadian poet Anne Carson, and the economics prize closes the season Monday.
