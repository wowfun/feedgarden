## Article

Michael Nicolls, SpaceX's vice president for Starlink and president of xAI, used a Tuesday presentation at the International Astronautical Congress in Antalya, Turkey, to call for better data-sharing among satellite operators, saying space can host more megaconstellations only if operators disclose where their satellites are and where they are going.

Starlink now operates more than 11,000 satellites, roughly two-thirds of all active spacecraft in low-Earth orbit, making it by far the largest constellation. SpaceX has also revealed plans for a constellation called Starmind of up to a million satellites to provide AI computing capacity in orbit; Amazon is in early deployment of a competing network of thousands of satellites, and two Chinese megaconstellations are in full-scale deployment.

Nicolls said collision risk on any given day is "fantastically low" and that SpaceX and most Western operators de-orbit rockets and satellites at mission end, but added that "the law of large numbers says that if it can happen, it will happen." No global treaty or regulation requires satellites to broadcast their locations and maneuvers or mandates coordination, so norms fall to governments and companies. He described debris as obstacles that can be tracked, measured and avoided, but said operators that maneuver without notification cannot be avoided, citing Starlink examples of other satellites changing trajectory without publishing ephemeris data or accounting for others' — producing conjunctions of tens to hundreds of meters, "way too close to comfort." He said the examples did not suggest hostile intent.

These are commenters' claims, not findings of the article.
