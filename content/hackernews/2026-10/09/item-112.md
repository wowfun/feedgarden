## Article

Fleeting is a site that plays long, realistic recordings of invented meetings so that anyone nearby assumes you are busy. Its stated premise is "workplace self-defence against people stealing your time": pick a meeting, press play, and let the background noise do the talking.

Users choose a meeting and can set their name and accents. They can also "put a fake meeting in your diary so you look busy before anyone asks"; the invite links back to the site, so the reminder gives them something to join. The page separately advertises paid placement for products featured inside a fake meeting.

On mechanics: each meeting runs about twelve minutes and starts at a random point, so the same opening line is not heard twice in a row. To end convincingly, the user presses "Leave" or "I have to drop," and the other participants say goodbye before the call ends. With "Back-to-back meetings" ticked, once the whole meeting has played everyone says goodbye and, a few seconds later, the user joins another one suited to the time of day; unticked, the meeting loops instead.

The site says no real people or meetings are involved: every voice is synthetic, faces are AI-generated or licensed stock footage, and every name is invented except John Carroll, who made the site and sometimes sits in with his camera off. To hide the address bar, the call goes full screen on a computer &#40;F toggles it&#41;, while on a phone the browser's "Add to Home Screen" option runs it full screen like an app.

## HN discussion

Based on 9 selected comments and replies.

Commenters say the demand already existed organically. One recalls sorting a secondary GitLab YouTube channel by views and finding an apparently ordinary, uninteresting meeting video with millions of views, used by people pretending to be busy; saved comments included "I play this when I need my family to leave me alone." A former manager describes telling an SRE team to create a weekly 8am–11am Friday "team meeting" to protect focus time, saying it carried more weight coming from a manager than from the team itself. Another did something similar using an "&#91;IMPORTANT&#93; CUSTOMER OUTAGE" placeholder, and criticizes senior colleagues who booked over existing meetings without a polite ask. Others doubt the illusion: the clips never overlap, each stopping before the next begins, and synthetic voices sound too clear to be organic. One warns a loud ad will break the spell; another simply finds the scripts funny and not inaccurate.
