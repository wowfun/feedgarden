## Article

The Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize to Navanethem "Navi" Pillay, citing exceptional courage and integrity in leading the way toward a more comprehensive global legal order and her role in ensuring that war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide are prosecuted. The announcement is dated 9 October 2026, and the committee notes that the first peace prize was awarded 125 years ago, when it already stressed resolving conflicts through international law.

Born into a family of Indian Tamil origin under apartheid in Durban, South Africa, Pillay became a lawyer and legal pioneer confronting structural discrimination, segregation and exclusion. She defended Nelson Mandela and others who opposed apartheid early in her career, then served as a judge on South Africa's High Court, the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda and the International Criminal Court. She was United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights and, until recently, chaired the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory. She is currently a judge at the International Court of Justice in the case where Myanmar stands accused of genocide.

The committee credits her with enlarging the scope and impact of international law and strengthening its institutions, calling her independence, expertise and steadfastness central to her reputation. It frames the prize as a reminder that the rule of law — national and international — underpins the system built to ensure peace and resolve conflicts non-violently, arguing that lasting peace must rest on legal principles rather than on the will of the strong or a fragile balance of power. It acknowledges the rules-based order has never been perfect, with the greatest powers often evading responsibility and outcomes varying by perpetrator and victim, and warns that international law is under tremendous pressure amid a shift toward power politics. "Peace requires justice," the committee concludes.
