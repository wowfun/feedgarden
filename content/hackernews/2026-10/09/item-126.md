## HN discussion

Based on 8 selected comments and replies.

Commenters largely attack the headline rather than the study. One points out the paper is actually "Structured Exercise after Adjuvant Chemotherapy for Colon Cancer," so the news framing overstates a narrow colon-cancer trial as a general claim about cancer survival. Others question selection effects, asking whether participants who could not keep up with the prescribed exercise were excluded from the exercise arm, which would filter for healthier subjects.

A reply reports reading the paper: physical functioning was slightly higher in the control group at baseline, protocol completion differed little between arms, and the exercise arm's survival looked unexpectedly high versus typical non-study rates. Another commenter notes the composite endpoint of recurrence, new primary cancer or death includes all-cause mortality, so some benefit could reflect fewer heart attacks and strokes.

Personal experience adds nuance: one commenter's mother took up running years after a terminal breast cancer diagnosis but saw many younger, fitter friends die of the disease. Another argues exercise is basic self-care for everyone. A further commenter stresses how hard longitudinal studies are given confounders like the gut-brain connection and autoimmune responses.
