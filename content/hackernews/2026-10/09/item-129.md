## Article

Oxide Computer announced a &#36;445M Series D round. The company frames the raise around a milestone reached in the spring: it paid income tax, not because of an unusual transaction but because ordinary operations — selling computers — produced taxable income after accounting for components, manufacturing, salaries and other costs. The post notes that most startups do not pay income tax because most are not profitable, and describes profitability as a lagging indicator of product/market fit.

Oxide says it also holds a very large order backlog. In a rapidly growing hardware business, cash must be committed to components and manufacturing well before systems reach customers, so demand far exceeding supply requires significant working capital. The company says its Series B, Series C, existing debt facilities and internally generated cash would have let it satisfy its current backlog, but at volumes that would have required real caution in accepting additional demand.

Series D was led by existing investor Eclipse, with existing investors USIT, Riot Ventures and Jane Street taking big pieces and Friends and Family Capital and Counterpart also joining. Atreides Management joins as a new investor, having first engaged with the company nearly two years earlier, and AMD joins as a new strategic investor. Oxide notes AMD's long support, including help achieving platform enablement software, and that AMD EPYC was one of its early bets.

The post says the Series C of &#36;200M was intended to entirely de-risk the company with respect to capital and to assure longevity and independence. The Series D is presented as building on that assurance to satisfy the backlog while continuing to accept new demand, expand manufacturing capacity and invest in an enduring company. The announcement is written from the company's own perspective and does not state revenue, margins, valuation, backlog size or specific terms.

## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters broadly welcomed the raise and praised Oxide's communication style, with one noting the photo caption about being "as excited as you can be paying taxes" as an example. That praise drew a challenge: one commenter asked why a company with a huge order backlog would pay taxes rather than plough money into expanding production, citing Amazon's long stretch of paper losses during relentless expansion.

Several commenters debated the equity-versus-debt choice. One said they were surprised Oxide did not use trade finance, since debt could cover customer orders, and speculated about whether the company is locking in orders from AMD and other suppliers beyond its current backlog; a reply argued debt can bankrupt a company while equity cannot, and that equity avoids the risk of customers cancelling orders during a downturn.

Two commenters discussed hiring. One described spending a great deal of time on an application and hearing nothing for months before rejection; the reply said the company is small and talent-dense, that most hires likely come through word of mouth rather than the website, and that applicants probably sense in advance whether they fit. Another commenter wished for a smaller 10-inch homelab mini server from Oxide.
