## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters dig into the Theranos archive rather than the site itself. One points to the correspondence with Tyler Shultz, the insider who confronted the fraud: only about four exchanges, all on the same day, 11 April 2014, and highlights that Shultz took on the company despite family and former attorney generals and military officers turning against him. The same commenter notes that Sunny Balwani and Elizabeth Holmes seemed to suspect Shultz or the CLIA lab director, Adam, of plotting against them, even as regulators who did their jobs saw the scam.

A reply admits that after reading Bad Blood and watching The Dropout, it still isn't clear how Holmes and Balwani fooled experts inside their own company for so long, since fooling the public is easy but fooling people working on the machines is harder, even accounting for micro-tasked work and an atmosphere of suspicion and deference to authority.

Another quotes a 26 October 2015 message from Holmes's Sent mailbox describing Theranos as a revolutionary business founded and led by a remarkable engineer and businesswoman; a reply notes that four lines pass before the last two words hint at what the company actually did. One commenter recalls when the site was devoted to fawning adoration of Holmes long after the fraud was exposed. Another calls the site a throwback to Adobe Flash-era promotional pages, and a reply laments that the old Space Jam website now just links to a trailer. A separate thread spins out a fantasy of networking lidar scans, drone point clouds and iCloud backups into an immersive clone of one's life.
