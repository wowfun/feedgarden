## Article

The Israeli government is promoting an online film that mimics the style of NAZA, an award-winning documentary about Israel's mass killings of civilians in Gaza, in an attempt to counter the real film's findings. NAZA was directed by the Oscar-winning film-makers Yuval Abraham and Rachel Szor and produced by the Guardian; it is in cinemas and due to be shown in more than 50 countries. The Israeli government has denounced the film and announced plans to strip Abraham and Szor of their citizenship, accusing them of treason.

In recent days Israeli embassies have promoted a link to a hastily produced online film titled NAZA: The Real Story, hosted at nazadocumentary.com. It mimics some of the style of the real documentary, including anonymised interviews shot on rooftops in Tel Aviv, and claims Israel has the "most moral army in the world". Ariel Averbuch, an Israeli attorney and army reservist who made the new film, described it as an "offensive move" intended to shift the world's focus from allegations about the Israeli military's killing of civilians. Another film-maker listed on the site is Roi Danino, a candidate for the rightwing Israel First party.

The domain was registered on 16 September, six days after NAZA's premiere at the Venice international film festival, where it won the special jury prize after receiving the longest ovation in the festival's history. The website initially falsely presented itself as a promotional page for NAZA itself, naming Abraham and Szor as directors and listing festivals where the documentary was due to screen. A synopsis on the page said the film "reveals in detail the systems behind Israel's calculated mass killing of terrorists in Gaza", distorting the official synopsis by removing the reference to the deaths of Palestinian civilians. That wording was removed in later updates once the rival film's video was added. The page now acts as a platform for the rival film with a counter suggesting it has been watched more than 42m times, while the film's official YouTube page is listed as having just 30,000 views; a technical analysis suggested the discrepancy could mean the counter was driven by requests to the website, by humans or bots, rather than actual viewership. Asked about the 42m figure, Averbuch said he did not know and referred questions to his tech team.

The Israeli military has killed more than 73,000 Palestinians in Gaza since the Hamas attacks of 7 October 2023, in a campaign a United Nations commission has described as genocide. The original film's 24 interviews with Israeli military insiders detail how an AI-assisted mass surveillance apparatus was activated during the Gaza war, revealing systems used to target Hamas operatives knowing large numbers of Palestinian civilians would also die. The Israeli embassy to the US, which shared the new documentary on social media, said the foreign ministry was not involved in producing it and that it intended only to "amplify the message of its creators and expose wider audiences to arguments challenging and dismantling the blood libels and false allegations". The Israeli government said it was awarding the film-makers a certificate of appreciation "for their contribution to Israeli public diplomacy", claiming the production had garnered tens of millions of views.
