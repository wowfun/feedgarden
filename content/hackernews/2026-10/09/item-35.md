## Article

The author, a long-time creative, describes a deteriorating relationship with Adobe: the company ignored Mac design conventions, made subscriptions its only real option, kept raising prices, and oriented its offerings toward customers buying hundreds of licenses a year. Power users migrated to Linux largely unnoticed, and Adobe's AI features often arrived against creatives' stated wishes.

That context explains the appeal of ArtCraft, an open-source, clean-room rebuild of the Creative Cloud suite produced in a "vibe-coded" workflow. The apps map onto familiar tools — stand-ins for Photoshop, Premiere Pro, Acrobat and even InDesign — with unfamiliar names. Built in Rust and distributed as AppImages, they opened fast and stayed small, loading in tens of megabytes rather than hundreds, detected the author's discrete GPU immediately, and allowed image editing within a couple of minutes.

Rough edges remain. Color pickers and secondary menus felt inelegant. DesignCraft did not detect the author's fonts until an update published minutes earlier was installed, and a hex color field available in PhotoCraft was missing in DesignCraft. The author still found the tools familiar enough to imagine building a zine or opening old files that otherwise require a subscription, and notes the project moves quickly.

The author argues such projects are worth cheering even for people who dislike AI, because they respond to real ethical and economic complaints about Adobe and because the techniques they hit upon may feed more craft-focused open-source work. He also states reservations: he thinks "craft" is a poor name for the project, given that the term implies heart that the current iteration does not yet evidence. The excerpt ends mid-sentence.

## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

- One commenter argues the clones are not worth cheering because they "suck the oxygen out of the open-source ecosystem" that more credible projects such as GIMP, Photopea or FreeCAD need; a reply disputes that framing, notes Photopea is actually proprietary, says the current build is somewhat unusable but could improve with time and tokens, and asks why GIMP has had decades to gain traction.
- Another frames the project as a bold experiment worth watching: whether large applications can be built by pure vibe coding, and whether resulting architectural rot will be better or worse than human-authored architectures, which also rot. He says early evidence leans toward "can you not," but the experiment is not over.
- On tooling limits, one commenter says getting a complete working product out of an LLM is hard — around 60/40 at best, rising to roughly 75–80 with extra tricks — using cheaper Chinese models and &#36;20 US plans. A reply argues rigorous specs do not help because they demand more context than models can respect, and that only specifications encoded as tests work reliably, which still requires detailed code understanding.
- A further commenter calls the project vaporware, claiming its author promotes it heavily while it is far from feature parity. The sample is small and not representative.
