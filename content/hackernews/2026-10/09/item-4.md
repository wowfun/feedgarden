## HN discussion

Based on 6 selected comments and replies.

The submitter asks whether anyone is building a positive standard for verifying that text was written by a human, arguing the labs' side will never work but that willing authors have an incentive to collect evidence voluntarily.

Commenters question the framing. One points to C2PA as an adjacent specification, which the submitter says they will review. Another argues "standard" needs far more detail, since any protocol is only as good as what a party feeds into it: even sealed, streamed keyboard events would only raise the bar for fraud, as a frontier model could generate a script imitating human typing speeds in under half an hour. A third commenter objects to the burden falling on people who write their own work, asking why they must prove they are not AI rather than the reverse, and noting a technology is not beneficial if it demands more effort than before.

In replies, the submitter cites watermarking being reverse-engineered and tools open-sourced to strip it after one vendor's announcement, and suggests either a YubiKey-style keyboard or a standardized origin system that publishing platforms can check against would be needed. Dissent and open questions remain unresolved in this sample.
