## Article

Tech Policy Press fellow Chris Mills Rodrigo reports that McClatchy announced a deep round of layoffs across its 30-newspaper chain while saying it remains committed to local journalism. The article argues the cuts and the company's simultaneous AI investment are hard to reconcile.

The numbers are stark. At the 162-year-old Idaho Statesman, 10 of 18 reporters went, along with three of four editors, leaving a staff of nine; a reporter and union chair said workers were given no roadmap for the future. McClatchy cut seven positions across its four Washington papers, including the reporter covering Tacoma city hall and local schools. The Tri-City Herald lost its only Spanish-speaking reporter. In Florida, the Miami Herald lost its city hall reporter and El Nuevo Herald's writing staff was gutted. In total at least 90 unionized journalists and media workers were affected, before counting editors and cuts at 13 nonunionized papers.

- One city hall reporter said it is hard to read the Miami cut as anything other than a pivot away from journalism.
- NewsGuild President Jon Schleuss called the cuts devastating for communities, noting many affected were accountability journalists.
- McClatchy did not cite financial headwinds; it told the Idaho NewsGuild that inability to pay wages was not the reason.

What makes this round distinctive is what the company is funding instead. Earlier this year McClatchy introduced an AI tool it calls a "Content Scaling Agent," used mainly to produce summarized versions of staff-written articles. Reporters say the rollout caught newsrooms by surprise, and at the Sacramento Bee they learned of it only when an editor asked a reporter to review an AI-generated summary.

The article notes McClatchy was bought by private equity firm Chatham Asset Management for &#36;312 million in 2020 after its previous owners declared bankruptcy, and that a company spokesperson did not respond to requests for comment. Context on the industry's broader print-advertising collapse is offered as background, not as the company's stated justification.
