## Article

Scientists using the European Space Agency's Mars Express, combined with a state-of-the-art meteorological model of Mars, have found that exotic physics may lie behind the Red Planet's most curious cloud. The Arsia Mons Elongated Cloud &#40;AMEC&#41; appears each spring and summer in Mars's southern hemisphere during the dusty season: a white wisp of water ice that emerges downwind of the 20-km-tall Arsia Mons volcano, forming, growing and fading daily, stretching up to 1,800 km — nearly twice the length of the UK — before quickly evaporating. The cycle repeats every morning for several months. Mars Express first revealed the cloud in 2018.

Researchers had already classified the AMEC as an orographic cloud, the kind seen on Earth that forms as wind flows past craggy topography. But simulations failed to reproduce what the images show until the team included physics that textbooks treat as theoretical and generally not expected to occur in nature. "Once we included this physics in our simulations, the AMEC emerged just as we hoped," said lead author Jorge Hernández-Bernal of LMD/CNRS/Sorbonne Université in Paris.

Normally clouds form when moist air cools and vapour condenses onto specks of dust, salt, pollen or soot — heterogeneous nucleation. For the AMEC, the vapour appears to turn directly into icy cloud particles with no such middle step, a process called homogeneous nucleation that the team says has never been seen before in a planetary atmosphere. It requires exceptional conditions, including relative humidity levels more than 100,000 times those experienced in daily life on Earth. Winds flowing past Arsia Mons trigger a powerful wave that lifts moist air parcels several kilometres in minutes, dropping temperatures by 30 degrees in 10 minutes and spiking humidity, after which vapour spontaneously freezes into cloud particles.

The findings suggest Mars's humidity can reach these extreme levels, a condition not previously observed there. Some aspects of the modelled cloud still do not exactly match observations, though the lead author calls the result remarkable.
