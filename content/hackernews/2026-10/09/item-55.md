## Article

President Donald Trump posted on Truth Social on Thursday that "The White House considers anyone that uses the term, 'Artificial Intelligence,' as opposed to the highly accepted new and more accurate term, 'Super Intelligence,' THE ENEMY&#33;" The statement suggests the White House could target companies or individuals who do not adopt its preferred wording, which the article notes would be a potential textbook First Amendment violation if enforced.

The White House has moved to rebrand artificial intelligence, which Trump said suggests the technology is "fake." After meeting industry leaders at the White House in late September, Trump signed an executive order requiring federal departments and agencies, "to the maximum extent permitted by law," to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" in official correspondence, public communications, websites and other official communications. The order did not apply to private-sector companies such as OpenAI, since the White House has no legal authority to enforce such a measure.

Some branding preceded the move: Meta, for instance, has a SuperIntelligence Labs unit, though its descriptions framed super intelligence as a future of AI rather than a rejection of the term. Since Trump's statements and orders, at least one company has complied. SpaceX CEO Elon Musk wrote on X that the company would rename its AI unit from SpaceXAI to SpaceXSI. If carried out, that would be the second rebrand this year for what was originally xAI: SpaceX announced in February that it was acquiring xAI, makers of the Grok chatbot, and renamed the unit SpaceXAI in July. As of Thursday afternoon, the SpaceXAI X account still read "SpaceXAI."

The reception among industry leaders was mixed to awkward. At a White House panel discussion, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang corrected himself after saying "AI" instead of "SI." Standing feet from Trump, OpenAI President Greg Brockman said the company "builds artificial intelligence" to help everyone. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman told the Politico Decoded podcast there were "no plans" to rename the company, adding that it has been talking about super intelligence a lot and will continue to do so. It remains to be seen what other leading AI and tech companies will do.
