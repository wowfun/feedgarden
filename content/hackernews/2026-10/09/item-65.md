## Article

Biohub, the U.S. Department of Energy, the National Institutes of Health and new funding partners announced an expansion of an international effort to generate and openly share data for predictive AI models of biology, totaling &#36;1.8 billion in funding, data, computation and new measurement technology. The announcement, dated October 7, 2026 from Redwood City, describes it as the largest coordinated commitment to AI-ready biological data to date.

Split of the commitments: DOE will invest more than &#36;500 million over five years in lab measurement, modeling and computation through the cross-agency Genesis Mission; NIH will coordinate relevant datasets, repositories and knowledge bases built through more than &#36;500 million in prior federal investment, and will work with Biohub to standardize them for AI model training. Google DeepMind, Isomorphic Labs and Meta are collectively investing &#36;300 million in the Virtual Biology Initiative. Biohub's founding &#36;500 million commitment anchors that initiative: &#36;400 million for new measurement technologies and &#36;100 million for research outside Biohub.

The technologies named include cryo-electron tomography resolving near-atomic detail inside cells, microscopy able to image millions to billions of cells in living tissue, and engineering tools to perturb biology at molecular, cellular, tissue and whole-organism levels. The stated goal is to expand cell response data to interventions across far more cell types and conditions than have been studied, and to build and validate tools for studying cells and interactions at greater scale, speed and accuracy.

Biohub Head of Science Alex Rives frames an accurate predictive model of biology as a way to run experiments digitally and unlock new paths to cures, calling the virtual cell one of the most important challenges of the next era of science. Isomorphic Labs President Max Jaderberg says the data needed to solve predictive systems biology exceeds what any single organization can produce, positioning his company as a founding member. The initiative invites the worldwide scientific community to join.
