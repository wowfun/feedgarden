## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

The submitted item has no article text, so this is only a synopsis of the supplied comments about the claim that public key cryptography may be lost.

One commenter &#40;50012724&#41; clarified that the widely discussed OpenAI math results replace two already efficient probabilistic algorithms with deterministic ones, and that neither affects encryption as deployed today — one is a quantum algorithm and the other concerns factorization over prime fields — adding that nothing in the past month confirms or disconfirms the stated view. A follow-up &#40;50013247&#41; found it questionable that frontier labs would simply publish cryptographic breakthroughs in a random batch of results.

Others defended the field. One &#40;50012729&#41; argued there is as much evidence for the opposite stance, since AI can help improve and formally verify security reductions, so long as mathematics permits some trapdoor function. Another &#40;50012828&#41; read the thesis as essentially a guess that P equals NP, and argued it is more likely that cryptocurrency implementations contain bugs outside the core algorithms that let coins be stolen without breaking the encryption. A further commenter &#40;50014249&#41; said they always chose much longer key lengths than recommended, and only shortened them when a longer option was treated as an unsupported separate algorithm, citing P-521.
