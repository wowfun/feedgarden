## Article

This Wikipedia article recounts the antitrust history of the DRAM memory industry and a renewed 2026 lawsuit.

In 2002, the US Department of Justice opened a probe into DRAM manufacturers after complaints from US computer makers including Dell and Gateway, which said inflated DRAM prices cut their profits and hurt their competitiveness in violation of the Sherman Antitrust Act. Five manufacturers ultimately pleaded guilty to involvement in an international price-fixing conspiracy between July 1, 1998, and June 15, 2002: Hynix, Infineon, Micron Technology, Samsung, and Elpida.

Penalties accumulated over years. In December 2003, Micron regional sales manager Alfred P. Censullo was charged with obstruction of justice for withholding and altering documents responsive to a grand jury subpoena served on Micron in June 2002, and pleaded guilty. Infineon pleaded guilty on October 20, 2004 and was fined &#36;160 million, then the third-largest antitrust fine in US history. Hynix was fined &#36;185 million in April 2005, and Samsung entered a guilty plea in October 2005. In April 2006, Samsung senior DRAM manager Sun Woo Lee plea-bargained, receiving 8 months in prison and a &#36;250,000 fine; he was later promoted to President of Samsung Germany in 2009 and President of Samsung Europe in 2014. On May 19, 2010, European regulators fined nine semiconductor manufacturers more than €331 million over the same 2002 cartel: Samsung, Infineon, Hynix, Elpida, NEC Electronics, Hitachi, Toshiba, Mitsubishi Electric and Nanya Technology. Micron received immunity for whistleblowing.

Later allegations had a mixed record. A Hagens Berman class action filed April 27, 2018 against Samsung, Hynix and Micron alleged price fixing pushed prices up through 2016 and 2017 — a period when DRAM prices nearly tripled between June 2016 and January 2018 and consumer PC RAM kit retail prices roughly doubled. The district court ruled for the companies, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed in March 2022, finding the plaintiffs lacked sufficiently plausible evidence under the Sherman Act.

On June 25, 2026, Marc Garciaguirre filed a class action in the California Northern District Court alleging the three companies coordinated a production shift from DDR3/DDR4 to HBM, causing prices to rise by 700% over four years.
