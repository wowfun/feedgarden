## Article

This post, adapted from Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian's keynote at Gemini at Work 2026, announces the Gemini agent: a single, universal agent for work.

According to the announcement, Gemini answers questions, handles knowledge work, creates images and media, and writes and runs code through one agent and one API. Inside Workspace it works directly in Gmail, Drive, Docs, Slides, Sheets, Chat and Calendar, carrying the same memory, skills and controls everywhere. Users are meant to give objectives rather than instructions, delegating an outcome and returning to finished work; the agent plans the work, uses skills and tools, connects to systems, and can act as a personal assistant, as a team member such as a project manager, or on behalf of a specific role such as a finance analyst.

The described architecture rests on three principles. It is a unified agent that can chat, work autonomously on assigned objectives, and generate code from one interface, with tasks schedulable or triggered by events. It offers omnipresent access from web, iOS and Android, Windows and Mac, and channels including command line, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Slack, and can run as a headless agent in third-party applications. It provides persistent execution in the cloud, maintaining a single set of memories, context and personalization graph across devices, so users never re-brief it.

The announcement also lists new data and analytics skills for plain-language operational insights, industry-specific specialization with tools, skills, connectors and knowledge for financial services and legal teams, and governance through identity and policy management, authorization and permission controls, secure sandboxing and network gateways. Cost control comes via multi-model orchestration, Smart Routing and real-time spend caps.

Adoption figures cited: in the last year nearly 500 Google Cloud customers each processed more than one trillion tokens; nearly 80% of all Google Cloud customers use its AI products, and nearly 90% of the Fortune 100 use Gemini Enterprise — which the post frames as organizations moving past experimentation.
