## Article

OpenAI told investors that it reached roughly &#36;50 billion in annualized revenue at the end of September, CNBC confirmed, lower than the &#36;68 billion figure widely reported late last month. The Financial Times first reported the &#36;50 billion number. A person familiar with the matter said the &#36;68 billion figure included gross revenue from OpenAI's partners, which helps investors compare OpenAI directly with rival Anthropic. OpenAI shared the update in an investor presentation, and also touted 77% total run-rate growth in its third quarter and 107% run-rate growth for its enterprise business.

AI-linked shares fell on the news: Nvidia dropped 3%, Oracle nearly 6%, CoreWeave nearly 8%, AMD and Broadcom 4% each, Intel 5% and Super Micro Computer nearly 5%. OpenAI is under pressure to justify its &#36;852 billion valuation ahead of a widely expected blockbuster IPO; it confidentially filed its prospectus with regulators in June, and executives have signalled a 2027 debut.

Anthropic is also preparing a major IPO and is reportedly seeking a &#36;2 trillion valuation; in August it told investors its annualized revenue run rate hit &#36;65 billion at the end of July. Reuters, citing a leaked prospectus, reported Anthropic's 2025 revenue was &#36;4.6 billion with a &#36;42 billion net loss. Independent research provider New Constructs called the offering the most ridiculous IPO of 2026 and valued the company at just &#36;150 billion.

Separately, OpenAI pulled plans to launch GPT-6.1 Astra, saying the model did not meet its safety standards, and CEO Sam Altman said in September that right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public. OpenAI is in early talks about a new round of roughly &#36;30 billion, with no term sheet finalized; it closed a &#36;122 billion round in March and CFO Sarah Friar said the company remains very well capitalized.

## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters focused on how little is disclosed about a company whose valuation underpins much of the sector. One notes that under past conditions OpenAI would have gone public long ago, so there is near zero information on how a roughly trillion-dollar business is doing. A reply argues that the scarcity of information is itself the most important information and speculates that OpenAI lacks long-term viability and is hoping for a breakthrough before funding dries up — an opinion, not a reported fact.

Others quote Financial Times reporting that the &#36;68 billion figure arose from investors grossing up OpenAI's number to compare with Anthropic's, since Anthropic includes sales through cloud partners such as AWS and Google Cloud while OpenAI does not; one commenter stresses that the gross-up was done by OpenAI's investors, not by OpenAI.

A further commenter asks what it is called to misstate revenue while taking money from investors, drawing a sardonic reply. Another reads the comparison as a sign Anthropic is growing faster, while the market still expects AI to absorb all software and services.
