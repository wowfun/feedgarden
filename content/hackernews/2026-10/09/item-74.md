## Article

Amazon is discontinuing the Fire tablet brand and replacing it with a new line of "Alexa Tablets," Amazon's head of consumer electronics Panos Panay confirmed in an interview with Bloomberg, as reported by The Verge. The new devices run standard Android rather than Amazon's custom Fire OS and gain full access to the Google Play Store, which Fire tablets never offered.

The shift addresses long-standing complaints. On Fire tablets, Gmail, Google Calendar, YouTube, Google Drive and other Google services were reachable only through the browser, because the official Google apps were not distributed in the Amazon Appstore. Panay told Bloomberg that the Fire line "wasn't giving customers what they were asking for" — which the article characterizes as access to Google services, better hardware quality, and less intrusive lock-screen advertising.

Amazon says it will continue to provide security updates for existing Fire tablets. "We won't leave any customer behind," Panay said, though he indicated owners should not expect further hardware refreshes of the Fire line. Panay also said more Amazon hardware announcements are planned through the fall, while ruling out an Amazon-branded computer or laptop.

Commenters questioned whether the rebrand actually serves Amazon's interests. One argued that Amazon's hardware business existed to funnel users to Amazon's own store and services, so a generic Android tablet with an Amazon skin and Alexa seems to undercut that goal, and wondered what the business value is.

Another pointed out that the limitation was never absolute: users could sideload the Google Play Store onto a Fire tablet using a well-known workaround, linking to a guide. Both comments are from a small top-level sample, so they reflect individual views rather than a consensus.
