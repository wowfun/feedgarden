## HN discussion

Based on 8 selected comments and replies.

Commenters pushed back on the essay's premise that audiences are becoming duller, and several criticisms focused on its form. One commenter noted that the article's example of excessive exposition, the Netflix film Irish Wish, is a roughly 90-minute work, and speculated that heavy recapping may reflect habits from multi-part TV, where reminding viewers of week-old plot points is less odd; they also cited actor Matt Damon's claim that filmmakers are told to repeat the plot several times because viewers are on their phones, which they found depressing.

A reply drew a historical parallel: Shakespeare reiterated key plot points because theater audiences were buying snacks, chatting and otherwise not fully attentive, and modern script editors cut those redundancies to shorten plays from four hours to two. In that view, adapting to audience expectations does not make those audiences dumb, and they were neither smarter nor dumber than people today.

Other commenters were more critical of the piece itself. One said the "simple/simplified" theme is repeated many times without much insight or exploration. A reply defended the essay as brevity in service of selling a longer book, and pointed to linked sources on simplification in song lyrics, melodies and sentence length in books. Another dismissed it as "neon yellow journalism and book promo," predicting the author has a newsletter. A final commenter said the perceived lack of subtlety is why they avoid mainstream TV shows, arguing there is nothing to engage distracted viewers.
