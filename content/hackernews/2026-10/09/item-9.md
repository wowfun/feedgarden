## Article

Anthropic published a new annual version of its Usage Policy, describing most changes as clarifications of existing rules and illustrating them with examples drawn from new capabilities and from misuse patterns observed over the past year in influence operations, weapons development and surveillance. The updated policy takes effect on November 12, alongside a Supported Regions Policy.

A new section, "Do Not Engage in Deceptive Campaigns or Artificial Activity," consolidates rules that were previously scattered across the elections, fraud, privacy and disinformation sections. It applies to deceptive activity of any kind, political or commercial, and covers obscuring who is behind a message, amplifying content through fake accounts or posts, and building tools and infrastructure for influence-operation campaigns; it responds to state media outlets, government propaganda offices and commercial firms using Claude to run fake account networks and fabricated news sites.

The elections section was sharpened and renamed "Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes," focused on deceiving voters or disrupting elections, including spreading false information about candidates or voting, impersonating candidates or officials, and suppressing turnout. The blanket prohibition on personalised vote and campaign targeting was removed because it also covered legitimate civic work such as nonprofits translating voter information or officials sending ballot cure notices; targeting that relies on deception or misuse of personal data remains prohibited under the deceptive campaigns, surveillance and privacy sections.

The weapons section now states explicitly that prohibitions extend to the software and components that make weapons work, and to actions such as arming drones and other autonomous vehicles. The surveillance and criminal justice section was rewritten following a September threat intelligence report documenting growing use of AI to build systems that identify and track political dissidents; tracking people without consent is prohibited whether done in real time or through analysis of previously collected data, and Claude cannot be used to decide or recommend who to investigate, arrest or charge. The update also adds controls for autonomous physical actions and addresses abusive behaviour toward the models, and clarifies requirements for high-risk uses in health and finance.
