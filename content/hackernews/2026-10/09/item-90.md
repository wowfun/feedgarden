## HN discussion

Based on 6 selected comments and replies.

The discussion centered on a transcription of the 1849 document and on how to read such records. One commenter supplied a full transcription: a receipt for six hundred dollars paid by Judge S. Williams for "Jane, a negro woman 18 years old and henry her child, about one year old, together with her future increase if any," warranted "sound in body and mind and slaves for life," dated Dec. 20, 1849, with a clause letting the seller reclaim the property if the money were repaid by Jan. 1, 1851.

A reply questioned why the dollar amount matters, asking whether it changes the inhumanity of the practice. Others shared related historical experience. One former law clerk at the Multnomah County Courthouse in Portland, Oregon, described finding a 1920s order in archived records for a man to be hanged in the public square because he was Black and had married a white woman. Another commenter stated simply that slavery is wrong; a reply agreed ironically, noting religious texts do not hold that view.
