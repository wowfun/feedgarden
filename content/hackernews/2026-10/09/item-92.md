## Article

Game designer Richard Garriott has confirmed he will soon regain the rights to Ultima, the influential RPG and MMO series he created, from Electronic Arts. He sold the series and his studio Origin Systems to EA in 1992 for &#36;30 million; EA released several Ultima titles in the 1990s, including the MMO Ultima Online, but canceled all of Origin's projects in 2000, and Garriott left the company. The last mainline entry was 1999's Ultima IX, and in the decades since EA twice attempted to revive the series as a free-to-play title, most recently with 2013's Ultima Forever.

The reversion rests on Section 203 of the US Copyright Act, which lets authors terminate past copyright transfers after 35 years. Garriott told interviewer Jesse Petrilla that this window for Ultima is months away and that he had already given EA notice. "There's no money that needs to transfer. All you have to do is tell them a year before you take it back," he said, adding that he notified EA in April and that the publisher replied it knew the law and would act accordingly.

Garriott also said he is in active discussions with EA about any Ultima projects they might work on together before the rights revert, which he expects will happen officially in 2027. In his account, the change at minimum would let him re-release the earlier Ultima games and potentially make sequels, giving him control over the future of the series.

Since leaving EA in 2000, Garriott founded Destination Games, which partnered with South Korean publisher NCsoft on the 2007 MMO Tabula Rasa, shut down in 2009 after disappointing sales. He also became a private astronaut, spending 12 days aboard the International Space Station in 2008.
