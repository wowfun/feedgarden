## Article

The Trump administration is suspending Microsoft, Adobe and several other technology companies from a program that helps skilled foreign workers obtain permanent residency in the United States, accusing the companies of fraud. Vice President JD Vance, speaking at a news conference reported by Reuters, said the message to Microsoft is that it is "a great American company" but must hire great American workers. Others suspended from the program include Capgemini, Cognizant, HCL, Infosys, Tata and Wipro.

Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling said the government would not accept any new or pending permanent labor certification applications involving these companies. H-1B visas are intended for highly skilled positions employers may struggle to fill with qualified US workers. Tech companies are among the largest users of the program; according to the Associated Press, nearly three-quarters of approved visas go to workers from India.

Microsoft responded in a statement, saying it believes in the strength and talent of the American workforce and that the vast majority of its US employees are Americans. It said that of roughly 6,000 H-1B applications it submitted in the last fiscal year, 80 percent were to extend or change the status of existing Microsoft employees rather than to hire new people, and that filings for new employees concerned people already legally in the United States, equal to only 1 percent of its US workforce. The company said it files H-1B petitions only for those meeting the visa category's rigorous standards and pays its H-1B employees the same as other employees doing comparable work. Adobe did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Vance also said the administration would investigate nine universities, including Harvard, Yale and Stanford, over allegations they abuse a program allowing international students to come to the US and use it to undermine wages for American workers.
