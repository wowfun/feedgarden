## Article

This is a guest post by Álvaro Lozano-Robledo on Terry Tao's blog, originally written in a different file format and converted with AI. Its stated TL;DR is to keep calm and carry on studying math. The author writes to undergraduate and graduate students who, only a few months earlier, were planning academic careers in mathematics but now see those plans as distant or impossible.

The post was prompted by a message from one such student, reproduced in full with permission. The student, who says he has been lost for months, asks two questions: whether math academia will be large and accessible enough for anyone with sheer passion, even without being the brightest in the field, or whether it will inevitably shrink until only the brightest minds can enter; and whether AI within five to ten years will surpass humans at pure math research. The author stresses the urgency of the message because students are making decisions now.

Lozano-Robledo says LLM advances are disrupting almost all aspects of mathematics research and education, and that his single greatest worry is the very real possibility of losing an entire generation of mathematicians. Students who a year ago were headed to graduate school are changing their minds, some considering careers as far from math as law school. He lists the questions they raise: whether AI will surpass any human's mathematical research ability; whether research mathematicians become professional prompters and interpreters of LLM output; whether only the brightest minds can meaningfully contribute; whether mathematicians will be employable or needed; and whether one should pursue a math PhD now.

The author begins with two disclaimers, the first being that his answers may age like milk, and notes the post reflects how he and many others in the community currently view the situation. The supplied text ends mid-sentence before he works through the questions.

## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters disagree about how students should respond. One urges them to cancel AI subscriptions and get to work, noting that hundreds of mathematicians, including Peter Scholze, have agreed not to use AI at all, since there is no reason to help train systems that make mathematicians' own lives harder. A reply describes a colleague's decade-long grant project, aimed at a habilitation, being done by a model in about 3.5 hours, and argues theoretical fields can substitute synthetic data for human data.

Another commenter highlights a blog comment warning that research publishing is drying up because answers can now be retrieved at will, making problems resemble homework with the answer in the back of the book. A student objects that such commentary fails to consider that AI progress may continue at its current pace, while students decide careers spanning decades now; a reply says planning forty years ahead is impossible and advises staying flexible and diversifying.

One commenter, a frequent user of frontier models, still expects human mathematics to be in demand beyond ten years, with 75% or higher confidence, and sees no jobs apocalypse. A reply counters that this depends on AI approaches hitting a wall, and asks what would be left for humans if AI surpasses them.
