## Article

Gentoo, the source-based Linux distribution built around the Portage package manager, relies largely on sponsored infrastructure and can only maintain a few vital machines itself. Many sponsors that backed the project for years have now stopped contributing, so Gentoo has issued a call for replacement infrastructure sponsors. Its sponsors page still lists names such as Oregon State University's Open Source Lab, CDN77, HP and Hetzner, but may not yet reflect the dropouts.

Gentoo's wiki lays out five ways to help, with preference given to supporters who share the project's vision of software that respects choice and stays open to user-driven changes:

- Donated machines for the project's US hosting locations; anything adequate for server-focused tasks today is acceptable.
- Colocation, where the sponsor provides rack space, power and connectivity while Gentoo supplies the hardware. Capacity outside the US is the most urgent need, though US offers are welcome.
- Virtual machines with at least 2 vCPUs, 4GB of RAM and 25GB of storage. The project leans on cloud where it can, but many services are monolithic and Kubernetes would suit them poorly.
- Full-package hosted physical servers, such as a Hetzner-style root server or a sponsor's older or unused hardware with full remote access for Gentoo. In the latter case the sponsor keeps ownership and hardware management, while Gentoo handles the operating system and remote management, asking only for advance notice before migration events.
- Money, with donated funds going toward colocation and hosting.

The article notes Gentoo is not alone in needing such backing: LVFS, the Linux firmware update service, leaned on the Linux Foundation and Red Hat, with Framework and the Open Source Firmware Foundation contributing &#36;10,000 a year, before Lenovo and Dell joined at the Premier tier at &#36;100,000 a year each, followed by HP and NVIDIA. Sponsorship inquiries go to Gentoo's infrastructure team at infra@gentoo.org.
