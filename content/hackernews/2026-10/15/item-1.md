## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters largely accept the decline and offer competing explanations. One attributes it to a long-running trend reachable to the 1960s &#40;cited via the book "Bowling Alone"&#41;, with media as the "simple answer": television, then the internet, then smartphones filled a hole once occupied by seeking company out of boredom. Another extends this to any connected device, recalling pre-pandemic Peloton rides and console sessions with distant friends — social life "moved" but became sedentary. A bar anecdote dates the shift to the iPhone's arrival, when a tablemate pulled out a phone instead of talking.

Cost is a contested factor. One commenter tallies a night out at &#36;300-500 once babysitting &#40;&#36;25/hour&#41;, dinner, tip and city parking are added; a reply calls that a defeatist worst-case scenario, arguing cheaper local options exist. Others describe the effort required: a self-described introvert treats initiating contact as a learnable skill rather than an innate trait, deliberately scheduling coffees and potlucks, while another says the trend may be worse elsewhere and among children who feel a void without screens. Several replies to the thread's root are also present.
