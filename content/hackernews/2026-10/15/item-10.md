## HN discussion

Based on 9 selected comments and replies.

Commenters dispute how the story is framed. One notes the submitted title is backwards, quoting the article's statement that Apple claims OpenAI and two former Apple employees, including OpenAI's now chief hardware officer Tang Tan, stole trade secrets, confidential product designs and proprietary manufacturing information. A reply distinguishes that earlier case from OpenAI's current claim about an ex-Apple employee sending Apple device information, calling the exchange tit-for-tat. Another criticizes the reporting as relaying an OpenAI PR statement and using charged language, arguing that being sued does not prevent someone from suing, so the two facts are unrelated. One commenter, citing Apple's history of colluding with rivals on illegal anti-poaching arrangements, says Apple does not deserve the benefit of the doubt. Others expect Apple is worried about a coming AI device, while one counters that Jony Ive's project has shown nothing in over a year, so Apple has little to fear.
