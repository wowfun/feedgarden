## HN discussion

Based on 4 selected comments and replies.

Commenters debated whether writing culture is dying at Amazon and what AI assistance does to it. A current team member described problems with AI-generated or heavily AI-assisted technical design docs: it is easy to produce very long documents exploring every conditional and perhaps catching more edge cases, but they sit at a lower abstraction level than a design doc warrants, sometimes to the point where the code might as well be pasted in, and they are harder to read. They noted an API-focused doc can reasonably include a service proto diff.

dwd-free replies brought experience and reading habits. A commenter who said they spent ten years at Amazon and left early this year defended the practice: writing culture is real and excellent when done correctly, because writing is harder than presenting bullet points for an hour, and it forces clear thinking before clear expression. They expected lower-level document reviews to fill with AI slop, but judged that handing a sloppy document to an executive would likely draw a sharp "what is this" and a swift exit from the room. Another compared skimming AI-era text to speed reading, which they never trusted because it loses details and comprehension, yet now they scan titles and section headings and skip low-density passages.

A further commenter said the problem extends beyond workplaces: searching for a good modern Python book turned up many releases tied to very specific Python versions that were AI-written and poorly reviewed, making good technical books in some areas hard to find. These are individual perspectives from a small sample, not a measured trend.
