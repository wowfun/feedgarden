## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

A recurring argument concerns who should protect such sites. nickff calls it absurd for the government to blame SpaceX for failing to secure its property against trespass and arson, especially if foreign-state actors are likely involved, since local police and national agencies would normally own that responsibility.

bobbyraduloff asserts, without documentation, that Bulgaria's interior minister, president and cabinet broadly avoid naming Russia as a matter of policy, citing Russian strikes on Ukrainian vessels near Bulgarian waters the previous day, and argues the logic of that stance pushes blame onto SpaceX instead. TrackerFF offers a different reading: rather than attacking NATO directly, Russia is said to be waging hybrid warfare in Europe to provoke a small retaliation that could justify a large mobilization, and has no interest in a real NATO fight.

senderista claims there is more sympathy for Russia in Bulgaria than in Poland, particularly among older people; flowerthoughts asks whether that sympathy targets the people or the state. matt3210 raises a broader worry that corporations are increasingly treated as legitimate war targets, noting that working for a corporation is not something one chooses to be a target for. culi notes surprise at the scale of ground infrastructure: alongside 11,127 satellites, over 1,500 ground antennas in the US alone are needed to run the network.
