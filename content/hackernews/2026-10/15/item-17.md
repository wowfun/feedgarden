## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters largely accepted the reported trend and shared personal experience rather than disputing it. munificent invokes Durkheim's concept of anomie, arguing that middle-aged men sit at the peak of social expectations of success during a period when success is hard to define or attain, as familiar norms are uprooted. monknomo agrees, noting that a once-stable career arc is now unpredictable, but takes comfort in the idea that a midlife pivot is historically common. happytoexplain, 40, describes anxiety dreams, increased drinking and, despite not being suicidal, more such thoughts than ever, pointing to AI's effect on career and hobbies, politics, and money. ryanbigg and gnarcoregrizz describe redundancy and a sense that developer skills have become less valuable as AI tools take over parts of the work; gnarcoregrizz calls himself relieved not to have a family. petecooper, who runs a UK men's suicide prevention nonprofit, says the average attendee at 200+ talking meetings he has facilitated is around 45, has had suicidal ideation more than once, and some form of survivorship bias applies. Several replies note alcohol's particular danger after 40.
