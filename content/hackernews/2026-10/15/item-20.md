## HN discussion

Based on 6 selected comments and replies.

Commenters frame the reported contractor hiring as an instance of Jevons' paradox: AI-driven development does not remove the work, it reshapes it, as in fixing flaws found by an AI. One jokes that the patches will themselves contain bugs, prompting another round of contractors and more bugs in turn. Another cites a remembered IBM study suggesting roughly one new bug appears for every two fixed, and a third calls the whole arrangement a recipe for spaghetti code.

A question runs through the thread: if the AI is good enough to find the flaws, why hire people rather than buy more tokens? The most detailed reply points to regulation and risk aversion in banking, where a single mistake can topple an institution within a day. On that view, the bank may have been comfortable using agents to read and analyze its code, but not to write it — leaving humans to author the patches. The comments are opinions and recollections, and no participant claims inside knowledge of the specific arrangement.
