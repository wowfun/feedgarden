## HN discussion

Based on 9 selected comments and replies.

Commenters split on why anyone would run Microsoft Defender on a Linux desktop. One argued corporate Ubuntu laptops were already smoother than Windows 11 machines on identical hardware, so supporting Linux is one way Microsoft answers that; another countered that most workplaces where Linux laptops are first-class still apply the same IT compliance stack as everyone else.

Several commenters questioned the appeal outright, with one calling it spyware installed on "the most secure OS by design" and asking why a user would ever choose it. Others pushed back: a commenter said they would happily run Asahi Linux on a work Mac but cannot without antivirus, anti-malware and DLP support, so broader Linux endpoint tooling is a step toward that. A reply to a "why would anyone" question suggested the real audience is WSL users in corporate environments, though commenters noted the press release and article do not say this.

Tone aside, the sample mixes skepticism about a Microsoft security agent on Linux with practical demand for corporate-manageable Linux desktops.
