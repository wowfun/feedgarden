## HN discussion

Based on 9 selected comments and replies.

Several commenters focused on the article's suggestion that the strikes did not take social media manipulation offline. One called that the buried lede, saying Cloudflare saw no evidence malicious activity was disrupted, which they said refutes the popular claim that Russian trolls went dark after the attack.

Others questioned whether Cloudflare's vantage point can support that conclusion. A commenter noted Cloudflare's limited relationship with major social platforms and argued bots exploit the gap between power users and average users, making them hard to distinguish. One commenter said the bots were already understood to be search engine crawlers. Another cited a BlueSky account that tracks bot and information-warfare accounts and reported no drop in activity, while allowing that the host may simply have good redundancy.

A different hypothesis was raised: activity may have quieted because manipulators relied, perhaps accidentally, on news-scraping bots for targeting, similar to incidental dependencies on a cloud region. Commenters also said they were more interested in the consequences of Yandex cloud being offline and whether ordinary Russian services suffered comparably.

The sample shows disagreement rather than consensus about what the outage data demonstrates.
