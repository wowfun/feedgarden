## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters debate whether Anthropic failed to credit prior art. One quotes Anthropic's announcement that source control for huge monorepos has prior art in Google's Piper and Meta's Sapling, and notes that Mononoke is a component of Sapling and that the linked repository points into the Sapling repo, so the complaint is unclear to them. A reply argues that crediting a general inspiration is not the same as acknowledging a near-copied engine, especially when an announcement reuses terminology the original designers coined. The submitter clarifies that Mononoke is the server-side part of Sapling as deployed at Meta, and that Scion appears closely inspired by it.

Others widen the concern. One commenter splits the problem into directly consulting an unattributed source versus independently reinventing a concept, and observes that many well-known systems are unknown even to practitioners. Another worries about routine "IP laundering" by LLMs, arguing that poor attribution will make people less willing to share ideas openly and will reduce diverse collaboration. A further commenter contends Anthropic could have used or extended Sapling/Mononoke and contributed back to the open source community, framing the choice as commercial rather than technical. Other threads raise agent-written code copying small GitHub projects and ask about the licenses involved, including whether they were permissive or strongly copyleft; these remain questions rather than established facts about Scion.
