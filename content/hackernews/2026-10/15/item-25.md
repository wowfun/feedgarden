## HN discussion

Based on 6 selected comments and replies.

Commenters read the case as a free-expression problem: one argues the West is "going backwards" by censoring voices and publications, and a reply extends that to Germany's laws against insulting politicians, invoking that country's past to question such restrictions. Others focus on why this particular group was designated. One commenter asks why Rooney did not simply support one of the many other pro-Palestinian movements that have not been marked by the UK's security apparatus. A reply answers that Palestine Action is a direct-action group that disrupts organisations trading with or supporting Israel, unlike groups that only stage street protests, and notes her stated communist and Irish background may align with it.

Other replies contest the headline's framing. One suggests the books could still be sold, but the publisher does not want the liability of having funded Palestine Action, and that selling books without paying the author raises a separate moral problem. Another calls the headline deliberately misleading, arguing she can sell books in the UK and only cannot direct the proceeds to a designated group, which she has said she intends to do.
