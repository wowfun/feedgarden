## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters framed the project as part of a broader pattern in hobby tools that do not chase an audience or paid plans: no logins, sharing through a plain URL, and self-hosting assumed. One user welcomed this as a democratizing force in software, even while arguing that AI reduces the power, influence and recognition of creators; a reply cautioned that you should not bet on such a tool still existing next month.

On the design itself, one commenter noted that the "tiny" constraint is significant because the whole document is encoded in the URL, which caps text documents at roughly 2,000 characters. Another pointed to a comparable presentation tool where both the editor and content are embedded in an HTML file, so the editor does not depend on the host site surviving.

Practical use was also discussed: one commenter said pointing a coding assistant at the project's llms.txt file let it generate URLs that render documents, and another described the "Trip Planner" app as intriguing, noting past group trips tended to become a mix of spreadsheets, saved map locations and scattered chatbot sessions because itinerary tools are not flexible enough.
