## HN discussion

Based on 9 selected comments and replies.

Commenters treat the link as another step in a broader complaint about Google's privacy practices. maxo133 argues Google is the most aggressive big-tech firm at deanonymizing users, citing QR-code human verification, reverse SMS verification, and requirements that a signing-up phone number match the country of a chosen VPN server. burningChrome describes carrying two phones — a near-empty "privacy" device and a second one holding maps, Gmail, banking and authenticators — while noting the two can still be tied together, and says friends question the effort when anonymity feels near-impossible.

declan&#95;roberts objects that phone numbers are now demanded by nearly every site, calling them "the social security number of the internet." kouteiheika replies with a worse scenario: being forced to add a phone number before an already-paid subscription can be used, with no way to delete stored card details and no reachable human support.

SoftTalker says the outcome was inevitable once one corporation controlled ad tech, mobile OS, browser, search and email; enjoyyourlife notes the description could equally fit Apple. rdtsc says he avoids Gemini and other Google services because a single misstep could lock him out of his main Gmail, leaving an HN front-page post as the only apparent recourse. MiroslavPokorny contends Big Tech shifts cost onto telcos while capturing the value of identifying every phone; alunchbox attributes the demand to sites relying on Google and Microsoft SSO instead of building their own identity systems.
