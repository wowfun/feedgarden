## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters split on whether rule by the over-65s is a problem. nyargh argues that while many older people remain sharp, cognitive decline begins in middle age and dementia risk rises sharply after 65, citing reported cases of impaired US senators still serving; tuesdaynight extends this to presidents born before the 1950s who "don't have skin in the game" and will not live to see their policies' consequences. CGMthrowaway jokes that "ten in ten children have parents who are older than them," prompting carabiner to ask whether wanting to be governed by peers is really so strange. avazhi counters that societies often value the wisdom of age, while Barrin92 disputes this, noting the trend is recent rather than historical and reading it as decadence or a geriatric electorate. monideas cites the Latin root of "senate" &#40;senex, old man&#41; as a structural feature, blaming Boomer upbringing specifically. Others ask what takeaway the article offers and answer in one word: "Ageism."
