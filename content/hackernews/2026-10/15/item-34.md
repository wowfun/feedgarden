## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters overwhelmingly reject the CEO's framing. datahack objects that no one voted for a crime-free society, that a private CEO has no authority to dictate how society is run, and that with violent crime falling a record 9.3% in 2025 there is no pressing crisis. theglenn88&#95; rereads the statement as "for me to make more money, I have to convince you to give up your privacy," and doubts surveillance would remove crime at all. AngryData notes that the most common crime is wage theft, which cameras would not address, a point tencentshill echoes sarcastically. joshAg argues he doesn't want a crime-free society because other priorities deserve the money and attention, and StevenWaterman agrees "the optimal amount of crime is nonzero." joshstrange adds that many past social advances, from women's and Black suffrage to unions and LGBTQ+ rights, were once criminalized, so a truly crime-free society would stifle change. syntaxing quotes Franklin's liberty-versus-safety line, while frankvdwaal counters that Franklin actually meant something narrower about taxation and self-government. fnordpiglet asks where the promised crime-free society is, since privacy has already been given away.
