## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters largely read the piece as a warning about how LLM use changes working life. One engineer says colleagues now ask an agent instead of each other; they would rather two engineers spend two hours on a bug than one engineer twenty minutes with an agent, because the slower path means exercising the brain, learning from a peer and bonding, and they frame efficiency as an executive concern. A reply pushes back that this assumes people did ask colleagues for help, and that many never wanted to bother anyone anyway.

Several commenters anchor the worry in credentials. One says they finished a PhD before these tools arrived and doubts they would have gained as solid a grasp of their thesis subject; another feels lucky to have twenty-five years of hard-won programming experience, since it lets them keep up with, correct and better direct tools like Claude Code, while also welcoming ideas they could never previously realise.

Others broaden the concern to collaboration and progress. One argues society benefits when in-person collaboration builds shared understanding, and that it is hard to see AI replacing that when people consume largely the same generated content, adding that alienation deepens. Another compares LLMs to the Sophons of the Three-Body Problem as a soft brake on human progress, conceding that opting out remains possible but unlikely.
