## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters treated the report as one more entry in a long-running pattern rather than an isolated case, pointing to Citizen Lab's earlier work and to a Wikipedia page on the Israeli cyber industry; one shared the Citizen Lab research link for the underlying report.

A recurring argument, stated most bluntly by one commenter, is that mercenary exploit vendors from this one country keep appearing in these stories — NSO, Candiru and now the firm in the article are cited as examples. Another commenter goes further, arguing that Israeli security firms should be presumed beholden to their government and therefore not trustworthy; a reply pushes the claim to the point of saying it would trust a Chinese vendor sooner, which is an opinion, not a finding in the article.

On defenses, one commenter argues phone privacy hardening and the death of some Android privacy features are beside the point, since a mercenary implant takes over the whole device and no messenger is safe. This is disputed in a reply: that view ignores cost and threat model, since paying a spyware vendor is far more expensive than a subpoena or a phishing email, and the attacker's effort is usually better spent elsewhere.

All of this is reader opinion about the article's subject; the item's article text was not supplied.
