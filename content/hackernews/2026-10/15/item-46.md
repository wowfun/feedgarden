## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters dispute the premise that mathematics has been upended by AI. One says nothing has really changed, that LLMs becoming superhuman at deduction was already priced in with o3, and that peer review, training and publishing need no overhaul beyond what a database and a website could already do. A reply argues the real change is in how academics earn recognition, and that proofs were never the most interesting part of mathematics, which is intuition and new questions. Another holds that LLMs only brute-force what existing knowledge technically permits, so human discovery stays valuable once they run out; that claim is challenged as unsupported speculation. Several read the essay as being about identity and economics rather than results. One offers a hypothetical in which AI "solves" a field but researchers keep working with UBI, saying they would not mourn; another says mathematicians have more identity tied to cognitive ability and social standing. One commenter argues the loss is the joy of being first to see a raw truth, and another dismisses the piece as demoralizing propaganda.
