## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters split over whether the "pelican riding a bicycle" example really supports a claim about diversity collapse in LLM image generation. One top reply argues the fault is the prompt: asked with no extra detail, a generic side-view pelican on nondescript terrain is exactly what should be expected, so the example does not show a defect. Another agrees that output looks like a boring but literal reading of the request, and that creativity should be asked for explicitly; a related reply counters that a nonsense prompt still carries unstated intent that the image ought to reflect, and that the real bug is the prompt failing to supply details. Several commenters instead treat uniformity as a genuine, underrated problem: post-trained models are said to be mode-collapsed relative to the post-training distribution, a failure mode seen across model types rather than unique to transformers. Others note shared biases even within diverse samples, such as almost every pelican facing left to right. One commenter frames low variety as a feature for coding, where boring, conventional code is preferred and creativity can come from the user.
