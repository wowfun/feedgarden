## HN discussion

Based on 9 selected comments and replies.

Commenters describe dating apps as heavily populated by bots and scam accounts, with some real human profiles mainly selling "content." One commenter relays a scam-investigator interview in which the hardest part is convincing victims they were defrauded: money is intercepted and returned, yet victims re-send it through alternate channels, insisting their loved one is not being paid. Another recalls scammers using convincing profiles to gather likes and paid superlikes, matching everyone back, then redirecting matches to Instagram or Snapchat; the paid superlikes are never refunded, and this commenter suspects a viable class action. A dissenting view argues scammers' rapport-building, trust-building and emotional-reading skills are rare and valuable, and could be used lucratively in legitimate work. Others ask why victims send so much money, attributing it to emotional manipulation and sunk cost, since each payment makes it harder to accept the romance was never real, and one proposes charging a penny per email to make mass spam and scams unprofitable.
