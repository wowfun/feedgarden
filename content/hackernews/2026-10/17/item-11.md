## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters broadly accept the finding but debate how to read it. One notes that "covered well" seems to mean "covered at all": a social-work task represented by 13 prompts cannot really be covered, and a strict definition might push the figure above 99.9%. Another invokes the Doorman Fallacy—automating an obvious task can remove other valuable functions—and a reply applies this to AI: hands-on work is rarely the bottleneck, since review, feedback and sign-off take days, and editing AI output is often difficult. Others describe Baumol's cost disease at team scale: far faster coding has not freed time, which now goes to presentations, Jira and QA; one says QA and code review consume about 80% of their time. A further commenter warns that O&#42;NET, the task taxonomy behind the analysis, misses many modern white-collar occupations and misstates their tasks.
