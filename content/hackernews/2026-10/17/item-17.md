## HN discussion

Based on 6 selected comments and replies.

Commenters largely treated the 2012 question as settled. One argued that motivated learners do better with MOOCs or self-study than by copying a lecturer's notes, and that universities' real protection is their power to issue certificates and degrees — a moat that would erode if graduates' earnings fell. A reply from someone building a university course that permits AI said in-person education is underrated: much of its value is soft, and technical knowledge now matters less than technical ability, which MOOCs may enable but do not necessarily build. Another commenter said years of MOOCs taught them a lot but raised neither employability nor pay. A further view held that government intervention in education and industry keeps propping up academia, with the hope that AI might change that. Others linked a 2013 HN thread on the same article and noted it came long before MOOC enthusiasm died.
