## Article

The developer, posting as &#95;wesley, has released Stoke Money, a personal finance app he built after finding that Personal Capital, Monarch and Copilot did not meet his needs. He argues that those apps treat monthly cash flow as the net of transactions, even though credit card charges appear immediately while the cash leaves a checking account only when the bill is paid the following month. That mismatch forced him to maintain a separate spreadsheet for actual cash flow. He also objected to budget-centric design, saying his spending varies enough that comparing justified expenses with a budget and repeatedly adjusting it until he 'passes' never suited him.

Stoke Money keeps accounts, transactions and categories, but centers on a cash-flow projection engine. Budgets are removed. Each income or spending category is projected from historical activity rather than as a flat monthly figure, and lands in the account normally used—checking for paychecks, rent and bills, cards for daily spending. Credit-card payments are modeled from the projected card balance, and users can add recurring transfers between accounts to represent savings, for example.

The app forecasts how cash moves and shows in advance when an account might run low. If every account looks healthy for the next 12 months, the author suggests, budgeting may not be the problem. The feature he values most is MCP integration, which he connected to Claude but says works with any AI agent; he has used it to import transactions from PDF statements, categorize and tag spending from a vacation, and plan transfers to cover expected payments.

## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

In this sample, commenters focused on trust and on whether forecasting solves a real problem. One argued spent money is already unavailable, so a later checking-account deduction does not change how they view cash flow. Another said they would not use the app without external validation of its security practices, calling encrypted access tokens table stakes and noting that comparable established products hold certifications; the developer replied that there is no SOC 2 yet and that he will clarify this. A commenter found no "VoidHQ" entry in California's business registry; the developer said Stoke is a one-person project and an LLC is being registered.

A GNUCash user described a similar setup using scheduled transactions 90 days ahead and a future-minimum-balance view, but said reconciliation across about 30 accounts, many without APIs, remains a major time sink.
