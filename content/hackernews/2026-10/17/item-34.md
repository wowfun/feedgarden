## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters first dispute the article's premise. A volunteer firefighter says his Kansas county's 911 center immediately maps cell callers by GPS and briefly tracks them after hang-up, while another replies that Bay Area cell calls have been routed to the California Highway Patrol in Vallejo without location information. Others debate alternatives for non-crime calls. One notes that 311 handles non-emergencies but that few places besides New York take it seriously. A commenter cites Eugene's CAHOOTS program, which ran from 1989 until recently: of roughly 24,000 calls in 2019, only 311 needed police backup, and the teams resolved almost 20 percent of all calls reaching the city's public-safety communications center, with, to his knowledge, no staff killed or seriously injured. Countering, others say police can escalate encounters yet are best equipped when situations turn dangerous, and that officers greatly outnumber paramedics.
