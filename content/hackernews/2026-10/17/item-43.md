Based on 8 selected comments and replies.

Commenters mostly trade observations about the snails rather than assess the article. One recalls the animals appearing suddenly in 1980s Bombay: after seeing only one or two, they were soon everywhere. Another asks how slow, vulnerable snails avoid predators; a reply says opossums and foxes do eat them, but the parasites they carry may teach animals to avoid them. The same reply notes that the snails can fertilize themselves, so a single individual suffices, and lay roughly 1,600 eggs a year in batches of about 200. Others quote the article's aside that the snails reach about eight inches but can stretch to nine for headline purposes, praise the headline and graphic, and drift into a tangent about the human botfly.
