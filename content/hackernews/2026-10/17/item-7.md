## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters sparred over how to read the survey. Aurornis highlighted the subheadline that the U.S. remains the top destination even as worldwide desire to migrate falls, arguing the decline is partly global. In reply, culi called that framing misleading, saying the worldwide drop is largely driven by the U.S., while Canada and Japan rose and the U.K. also fell notably. Ritewut said they want to leave the U.S. but find anti-immigration politics in other countries too, and wolvoleo described reciprocal visa rules, citing shorter stays for Americans in Australia than for Australians in the U.S. k&#95;&#95; asked whether deterring would-be migrants is the intended effect, saying ICE enforcement does not look inviting. rayiner noted that only about 10% of people in South and East Asia say they would leave even given the chance.
