## Article

Tech stocks slid for a second day as investors reacted to quarterly results showing massive AI capital expenditure but uneven near-term returns. Major hyperscalers including Meta, Alphabet, Oracle, Amazon and Microsoft are collectively committed to around &#36;800bn in AI-related spending in 2026, with Alphabet expecting up to &#36;205bn this year and Amazon around &#36;220bn. Despite those commitments, earnings reports spooked markets: Alphabet's stock fell sharply after revealing AI Overviews were affecting core search advertising revenue, wiping over &#36;500bn off its market value at one point. Intel saw the steepest drop, shedding over &#36;130bn in value after saying it did not expect to generate more cash than it spent in 2025 and guided for lower fourth-quarter revenue, citing a continued PC slowdown. Amazon also reported spending growing rapidly and cash outflows increasing. The selloff extended to Reddit &#40;down sharply on weaker daily users and AI-related referral traffic concerns&#41; and Roblox &#40;down on falling daily active users&#41;. Analysts cited growing pains in the AI buildout, investor demands for payback, and fears of an AI bubble as key drivers.

A key mechanism behind the reaction is the mismatch between infrastructure investment and monetization. For search-heavy businesses, AI-generated answers can reduce click-through to ad-supported results, putting pressure on established revenue streams even as usage grows. At the same time, capital intensity is rising: companies are front-loading spending on data centers, chips and networking before clear, scaled returns are visible. Market participants appear less concerned with long-term potential than with execution and timing, punishing any sign of disappointment in quarterly results.

Implications remain uncertain. The scale of commitments creates high expectations, and any slowdown in demand or monetization could trigger further volatility. However, the correction is also selective - driven by company-specific signals &#40;guidance, monetization pressure, user trends&#41; rather than a single macro shock. Whether this marks a broader AI bubble unwind or a healthy repricing remains debated; for now, investors are demanding clearer evidence that massive AI spending will translate into sustainable revenue.

Discussion focused less on raw AI spending costs than on competitive and structural pressures. Several commenters argued Google's AI Overviews are cutting ad click-throughs and hurting core search revenue, while Reddit's traffic drop may reflect both AI search and its own login requirements. Others warned of heavy leverage in the AI buildout, comparing it to past bubbles and noting that infrastructure tied to US projects may be hard to export if it unwinds, raising systemic risk. A few saw the selloff as reflecting disruption to business models &#40;agents and AI summaries reducing referrals&#41; rather than just capex levels, with skepticism about near-term payback.
