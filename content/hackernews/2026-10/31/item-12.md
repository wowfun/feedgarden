## Article

The Free Software Foundation has joined Bluesky to reach people beyond its existing free software audiences, even as it remains critical of the platform. The FSF acknowledges Bluesky has made some improvements over services like X, but flags persistent concerns: signing up still requires loading nonfree JavaScript, which violates user freedom; Bluesky uses its own AT Protocol rather than ActivityPub so it is not fully part of the fediverse and the project itself acts as arbiter of the protocol; and while much of the codebase is free, the web client still serves nonfree JavaScript. To work around the signup barrier, the FSF's tech team built a custom program to post via an existing workflow without requiring every staff member to go through the nonfree signup flow. The organization accepts that limited compromise as a trade-off to extend its education efforts, urging supporters on Bluesky to follow its account and help spread free software principles to those unfamiliar with them.

The discussion reflects a mix of amusement, playfulness, and practical skepticism about the FSF's move. Commenters poke at Stallman-style rhetoric with jokes about GNU/Linux naming and copypasta, showing a familiar culture of teasing around free software purism. At the same time, others raise more concrete questions about Bluesky's trajectory, noting its slow decline in user growth and pointing to once-useful tracking sites going offline. These reactions capture both the affectionate ribbing the FSF often attracts and a measure of doubt about whether joining Bluesky meaningfully expands its reach.
