## Article

Yves Couder and Emmanuel Fort's 2005 discovery showed that droplets walking on a vibrating fluid bath can reproduce behaviors long thought unique to quantum mechanics. John W. M. Bush's group has since pursued this hydrodynamic pilot-wave system through integrated experiments and theory, developing a stroboscopic model for walker dynamics and extending it to multi-walker interactions and boundaries.

The work revisits classic hydrodynamic quantum analogs such as diffraction, tunneling, orbital dynamics and crystal vibrations, while also uncovering new ones including walkers in corrals, Friedel oscillations, spin lattices and optical analogs like the Talbot effect. These studies point to the mean pilot-wave potential as playing a role analogous to Bohm's quantum potential, suggesting how classical local dynamics can produce apparently nonlocal effects. Resonance between droplet and wave, together with a lingering quasi-monochromatic pilot wave, emerges as the key mechanism.

Building on these features, the group has developed a generalized pilot-wave framework that bridges the macroscopic walking-droplet system with de Broglie's and Bohm's quantum pilot-wave theories, and has proposed a Hydrodynamic Quantum Theory as an extension of de Broglie's original program. The focus is not to claim equivalence to quantum mechanics, but to map which quantum-like features arise classically and what limits remain for such hydrodynamic analogs.
