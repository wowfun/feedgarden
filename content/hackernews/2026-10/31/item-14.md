## Article

In 1863, Jules Verne did something unusual for him: instead of sending heroes to an undiscovered island, the bottom of the sea, the interior of the Earth or the far side of the Moon, he looked directly into the future. He imagined Paris in the year 1960 and asked what sort of civilization the nineteenth century might become if its faith in science, machinery, commerce and efficiency were allowed to run to their logical conclusion. The result was Paris in the Twentieth Century, a book so unlike the Verne readers later came to know that his publisher, Pierre-Jules Hetzel, refused to publish it, judging the prophecy implausible and the tone too bleak. Verne put the manuscript away, and it remained unpublished for 131 years, until it was recovered from a family safe and finally appeared in France in 1994. The piece is by John Steele, publisher and executive editor of Blue Continuum, and is marked as a roughly 10-minute read.
