## Article

Archive of Our Own &#40;AO3&#41; remains down following the detection of a security incident. The team reports no works, bookmarks, or kudos have been deleted so far, and donor information held in a separate system was not exposed. However, they cannot rule out that the user database was stolen. That database may include usernames, email addresses, IP addresses, and a hashed version of passwords. While passwords are stored with industry-standard hashing, weak or reused passwords remain vulnerable to cracking. AO3 does not collect real names, limiting some categories of exposure. The team expects restoration to take at least two weeks and potentially up to four weeks. A degraded, read-only version may be brought online temporarily if possible, but it could need to come down again during recovery. Updates will come through official channels as the investigation progresses, with no expectation of individual responses.

## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters focused on the scope of potential exposure rather than the downtime itself. One pointed to the sensitivity of AO3 data, noting that a leak of usernames, emails, and reading history could be especially damaging. Others discussed technical mitigations, with a suggestion to move toward stronger authentication such as WebAuthn, though that was met with a reminder that replacing passwords with hardware-based factors carries tradeoffs. Several comments reflected broader fatigue with breaches, including concern that undetected incidents may be far more common than reported ones. A few also expressed empathy for those on the infrastructure side dealing with incidents like this.
