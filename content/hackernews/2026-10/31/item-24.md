## Article

A forum post describes a French Socialist-group amendment that would tax digital data producers, framed as restoring fairness between established businesses—whose tax base is physically located in France—and globalized digital firms that largely escape traditional taxation. The levy would be based on internet data transmitted to French users, measured by internet service providers without accessing content, at €0.055 per gigabyte, with an expected €10 billion in revenue. Most of the burden would fall on major international players such as Google, Meta, Amazon, Netflix and Akamai, which account for about half of the traffic measured by ARCEP; ARCEP estimates 200 billion gigabytes are sent to French subscribers each year. The post cites concrete impacts. VLC says the tax could cost it over €1 million per year even though it is non-profit, and Steam figures suggest French users downloaded 62.7 petabytes in seven days, implying roughly €3.5 million per week—or about €180 million per year—for Valve alone. The amendment presents the levy as non-relocatable, privacy-preserving and environmentally beneficial because it would encourage providers to compress and optimize data. However, one commenter states the measure was only a proposal and was rejected, so it is not enacted law. Discussion also raises a cap of 15% of French data-transmission revenue or 2% of global revenue, possible protectionist effects, and concerns that services could add fees or withdraw from France.

## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters disagree on the proposal's merits and likely effects. One calls it a hostile levy that targets productive activity without justification, while another notes it is capped at 15% of French data-transmission revenue or 2% of global revenue, suggesting a protectionist tilt and asking what would stop Netflix from spinning out a France-only entity to use the lower cap. A commenter warns France could become siloed, with providers adding fees or withdrawing, citing VLC's estimated €1 million annual cost and Steam's large weekly liability. Another sees a silver lining in discouraging bloated sites and autoplay video. One commenter states the measure was only a proposal that was rejected, not enacted law.
