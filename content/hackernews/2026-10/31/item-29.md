## Article

On October 1, we launched the prototype satellite for Project Suncatcher, a long-term research moonshot exploring the idea of powering AI compute in space. In the weeks since, the team completed a commissioning checklist that included powering up the host and TPU trays and running early machine learning tests. This week the project reached its “Hello World” milestone: the first-ever AI response from a data-center-class TPU in orbit. During a ground-station pass, engineers uplinked a prompt to the onboard Gemma 4 open model, asking it to generate a haiku about being in space. As the satellite passed by Svalbard, Norway, the downlinked poem confirmed success: “Blue marble below / Data streams in silent grace / Starlight feeds my core.” The team also tested space-themed limericks and telemetry prompts reporting the technical status of the payload systems. Next, Project Suncatcher will run tests evaluating how TPUs perform under thermal stress and radiation exposure in orbit. Verifying that the chips can reliably execute workloads in space paves the way for the next milestone: twin interconnected satellites in 2027.
