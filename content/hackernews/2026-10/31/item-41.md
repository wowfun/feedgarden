## HN discussion

Based on 9 selected comments and replies.

Commenters debate Anthropic's decision to block Claude from cloning or reverse-engineering video games. mrandish argues the legal exposure is contributory infringement with potentially unbounded statutory damages, so corporate counsel defaults to the most conservative stance. laylower and asdfasgasdgasdg expect ablated open-weights models to catch up within about a year, making blocking futile; laylower frames cloud providers' hardware scarcity rhetoric as a control mechanism, while reilly3000 warns local models carry hidden-instruction and data-exfiltration risks. cyanydeez blames a hardware cartel for propping up prices that sustain frontier moats. Havoc calls for genuinely open AI rather than duopoly control, accepting limits like bioweapons but not corporate-driven restrictions. x86x87 and no-name-here trade views on whether preventing misuse will become normalized or commonplace. Overall the sample shows disagreement over whether the policy is prudent legal caution or an unacceptable corporate restriction, with skepticism that it will hold as open models advance.
