## Article

Anthropic quietly added a rule to Claude Code's prompt—pushed around October 27 and listed under “tengu&#95;heron&#95;brook”—that cannot be disabled. It tells the model to build original games, games in a genre, and games from the user's own designs and assets, but not to recreate a specific existing game, even if renamed or called original. Addy Osmani, a member of Anthropic's technical staff, confirmed the change on X, saying using Claude to infringe IP is against the company's usage policy; original, genre and own-design games are meant to keep working, and Anthropic will iterate to strike the right balance. The move comes amid a flood of AI-driven game remakes in recent months, with users recreating classic and upcoming games for fun or profit. Independent developers fear fast-follow clones could beat copied games to market. Digital Sun Games cancelled a public playtest for its upcoming ReVamp over concerns someone might feed the demo into an AI tool and release a clone. Developers also pressed Valve on how to keep such clones from supplanting games on Steam, prompting a rare explanation to developers in South Korea. The rule may only deter low-effort copycats. Scientists and reporters have documented that generative AI safety limits can be sidestepped with rhetorical tricks. It is also unclear how the software can tell whether a user owns a game idea or its assets. And, as many users noted, cloners may switch to open-weight models that impose no such restrictions if Claude becomes too difficult to use. Anthropic was contacted for comment.
