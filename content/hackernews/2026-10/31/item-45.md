## Article

In California federal court, Meta faces lawsuits brought by authors including Sarah Silverman and Ta-Nehisi Coates and publishers including Entrepreneur Media and Chicken Soup for the Soul over the use of pirated books to train its Llama AI models. Judge Vince Chhabria is handling the cases together. A court already ruled last year that training Llama on books was fair use, so the remaining dispute centers on how Meta obtained the material, particularly through BitTorrent. The plaintiffs cite deposition testimony from former Meta researcher Stephen Roller, who worked at Meta from 2017 to 2023. Roller admitted torrenting Books3, a collection of nearly 200,000 books from the private torrent tracker Bibliotik, and knowingly distributing it. Meta had earlier told Entrepreneur that it did not believe it obtained Books3 through BitTorrent, and admitted in early 2024 that it used portions of the dataset for Llama 1 and Llama 2 without explaining how it was acquired. Roller also testified that Meta had copies of Books3 by the time he left its FAIR research group and named two research scientists who likely acquired their own copies. He deleted his copy about a year ago, possibly after a second subpoena, citing the litigation. Meta disputes this reading. It says the status report first raised these concerns and that Roller did not know when he downloaded “The Pile” via torrent or whether the download was for Meta rather than for himself or another employer. He also did not know whether more than one copy was downloaded or whether the named researchers acquired Books3. The deleted copy was on Roller's personal computer, removed more than two years after he left Meta in January 2023 because he no longer needed it. He has since worked for three other AI companies. The plaintiffs also seek an internal Google Drive folder titled “ML Books” and records of a Meta book club that may have used shadow-library books, including a document in which an employee shared a “pirated book” with a colleague. Key acquisition details remain contested and uncertain.
