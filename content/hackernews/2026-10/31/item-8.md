## Article

The poster asks whether applying to Y Combinator is worthwhile without insider connections or advantages. They note the potential upside of seed funding but worry it's a waste of time, observing that funded companies often aren't the practical, cashflow-positive businesses they want to build.

Comments suggest YC is fundamentally geared toward high-growth "rocketship" startups rather than sustainable, profitable businesses. Raising venture capital makes investors the customer, meaning founders must align with VC goals of rapid growth and outsized returns - which is often at odds with building a cashflow-positive company. Alternatives like SBA loans may be better suited for that approach.

Several commenters share firsthand experience: one made it to interviews but was rejected, ultimately relieved as VC funding didn't match their values. Others argue applying is low-cost &#40;the YC app is quick compared to other VCs&#41; so it's worth trying regardless of connections, though success is far from guaranteed and false negatives happen. The key question is whether the founder wants to play the VC growth game at all - if not, it's not worth applying regardless of insider status.
