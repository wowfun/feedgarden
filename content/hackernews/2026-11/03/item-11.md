## Article

Oracle filed plans on Monday to issue &#36;10 billion in common stock and &#36;10 billion in senior notes, raising &#36;20 billion to finance its artificial intelligence buildout. Although the fundraising had been previewed at an investor day the prior week, Oracle's shares fell 8.8% on Monday. The company said proceeds will go to general corporate purposes, which may include capital expenditures, debt repayment and future investments. The filing follows last Wednesday's investor day, where Oracle issued a revenue forecast of at least &#36;225 billion for fiscal 2030 and said it would limit additional capital-market funding for the fiscal 2027–2030 investment cycle to &#36;20 billion. UBS analyst Karl Keirstead called that limit a major relief given concerns about debt markets and the cost of capital, though he argued those concerns have been overstated. To reach positive free cash flow, which it expects in the second half of 2028, Oracle plans to rely on vendor financing, customer-provided hardware and customer prepayments. Oracle's stock and bonds have been under pressure amid investor concerns about the debt funding AI infrastructure. Shares are down 33% in 2026 and 50% over the past 12 months. Its investment-grade 4.95% bond due in 2031 yields 7.276%, and its five-year credit default swap spread sits at 292, up 265% in a year—levels more in line with a high-end junk rating. S&amp;P downgraded Oracle to BBB- in July, one notch above high yield, and the company carried &#36;125.34 billion in debt as of Aug. 31, mostly senior notes. Pat Nerney of Dynasty Financial Partners framed execution as the key question across the AI space: if Oracle delivers on its plans it will be rewarded, but if it must return to the market in the back half of 2027 after telling investors this was the last capital needed, the consequences could be severe.
