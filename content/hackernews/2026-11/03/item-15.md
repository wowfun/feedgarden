## Article

Chip stocks led a broad but uneven market decline on Monday: while about seven in ten S&amp;P 500 stocks rose, nearly every semiconductor name fell, and the group shed more than &#36;400 billion in market value in a single session, according to Yahoo Finance calculations. Intel led the decline again, sliding about 9% after its worst day since 1974 on October 30, when a disappointing forecast knocked nearly 29% off the stock; by Monday's close it was worth less than half of its end-September value. The damage spread beyond Intel to the memory and storage names that led this year's chip boom—Micron, Sandisk, Seagate and Western Digital fell roughly 6% to 8%—while Marvell dropped even harder as it agreed to buy Lumentum in a &#36;115 billion deal. Nvidia held up better than most but was still down about 16% since October 6. Zoomed out, chip and memory stocks have lost about &#36;3.7 trillion since October 6, with Nvidia the largest dollar loser and Intel the steepest percentage fall among the giants. Not all AI-linked names bled: SpaceX rose, though it remained below early-October levels, and software was mixed, except cybersecurity stocks such as Fortinet and CrowdStrike, which fell with chips. Analysts split on what comes next. Adam Johnson of Bullseye Brief argued last week's sell-off already priced in the worst, suggesting a bottom may be near. John Chambers of JC2 Ventures expects the gap between winners and losers to widen and warned of major train wrecks, while saying he would not bet against Nvidia. Jeff Hirsch of Stock Trader's Almanac, who bought the October 30 sell-off, noted chips have risen in all eight midterm cycles since 1994 from the last October close through the following June, though in 2002 and 2018 they first fell about another 11%. AMD's third-quarter results, due Tuesday after the bell alongside the midterm vote, are the next catalyst.
