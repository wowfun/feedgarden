## Article

Quilicura, Chile, sits within the Maipo River basin, one of the main sources of water for people and farming. Since the early 2010s it has become a major hub for AI data centers that use large amounts of water to cool servers. Combined with an ongoing climate crisis, Chile is facing a severe drought expected to last until 2040. The project contends that although AI can advance humanity, irresponsible use can have disastrous effects on water and other resources. It argues that someone casually using AI to create funny images in New York or Berlin can affect the water supply in Quilicura. To make that impact visible, residents of Quilicura became “human AI” for a day—answering prompts, creating content with local artists, and completing other tasks manually. The initiative sought to dimensionalize a complex problem and show the world the real people affected by irresponsible AI use and the hidden cost it imposes on local water resources.

Commenters debated both the project's advocacy and the underlying water claims. jrflo compared the water used by the human participants with AI, estimating people used roughly four times more water per interaction than a model, while noting the fate of that water matters. bko replied that data-center water is recirculated, but argued this misses “freshwater scarcity”—cleaning and redistributing water takes time and energy. vessenes praised the advocacy but disputed that AI data centers caused a drought lasting until 2040, attributing such droughts mainly to climate change. chrisdbanks suggested capturing waste heat from servers to heat water for public benefit. jimmyjazz14 called some claims propagandistic and argued the focus should be alternative cooling technologies. neom pointed to a broader Chilean water crisis beyond data centers.
