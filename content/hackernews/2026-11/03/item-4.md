## HN discussion

Based on 10 selected comments and replies.

Commenters discuss patterns behind US names shifting gender association. One notes that whichever direction a name moved, the largest change usually came from girls—increases among girls drove moves toward girls, while declines among girls drove moves toward boys—suggesting girls' names are more dynamic. Another suggests boys more often receive family or biblical names, concentrating popularity among a smaller set, while girls' names follow fashion faster. Others point out cross-language confusion &#40;Jan/John, Jean&#41; and immigration mixing similar names. A commenter observes that names ending in vowels often read feminine, with shortening over time complicating the rule; a reply adds Russian informal '-sha' names. Anecdotes include abuse for a boy named Hilary and a Johnny Cash reference. One commenter argues the article overstates by framing rare sometimes-shared names as popular boys' names becoming girls' names, using Ashley's growth among girls &#40;and a smaller rise among boys&#41; as illustration.
