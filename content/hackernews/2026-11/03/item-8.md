## Article

A first-person short story follows a developer through a morning routine—waking two minutes before the alarm, listening to a weather report, burning toast on one side of a toaster, ironing a shirt—while awaiting a Friday meeting about “roles” announced by a manager without emoji. The narrator reflects on a career arc from writing code to describing code to an agent, then to answering the agent’s questions, even though the employment record still reads “lead developer.” The piece suggests machines took the interesting work first, leaving humans with waiting, checking, and carrying a phone around. The narrator works for a flower-delivery service whose orders peak in the morning. For three weeks, three or four orders vanish around 8:14 a.m.: money is charged but flowers never arrive. The narrator’s agent traces the cause to a clock mismatch—server and warehouse clocks differ by about a minute, so an order falling in that gap is marked sent by the server but not yet received by the warehouse. The narrator calls it the “no-man’s minute.” Because the agent lacks warehouse access, he must inspect the order queue himself at 8:14. The text cuts off as he reaches a symmetrical courtyard whose face-recognition gate fails to identify him while he wears a hat.
