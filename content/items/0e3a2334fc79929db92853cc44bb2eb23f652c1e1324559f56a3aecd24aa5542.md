Anthropic published a new version of its Usage Policy, saying most changes clarify existing rules rather than impose new ones. The update responds to the past year's evolution of Claude's capabilities and to customer feedback, and takes effect on November 12. The company says the revised policy adds examples showing how its rules apply to longer, more independent Claude work, and draws on misuse patterns observed in influence operations, weapons development and surveillance documented in its latest threat intelligence report.

Key changes include:

- A consolidated section, "Do Not Engage in Deceptive Campaigns or Artificial Activity," gathering rules previously scattered across the elections, fraud, privacy and disinformation sections. It applies to deceptive activity of any kind, political or commercial, covering efforts to obscure who is behind a message, amplify content through fake accounts or posts, and build tools or infrastructure for influence-operation campaigns.
- The elections section is renamed "Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes" and now targets deception of voters or disruption of elections, such as spreading false information about candidates or voting, impersonating candidates or election officials, or suppressing turnout. The blanket prohibition on personalized vote and campaign targeting is removed because it also covered legitimate civic work, like nonprofits writing voter information in other languages or election officials sending ballot cure notices; the underlying abuses remain prohibited under the deceptive campaigns, surveillance and privacy sections.
- Weapons prohibitions are clarified to explicitly cover the software and components that make weapons work, and actions such as arming drones and other autonomous vehicles. Anthropic says this reflects how it already enforced the prior policy.
- The surveillance and criminal justice section is rewritten to state prohibitions more precisely, including that tracking people without their consent is prohibited whether done in real time or through analysis of previously collected data.

The policy also adds controls for autonomous physical actions by Claude, updates requirements for high-risk use cases such as health and finance, and addresses abusive behavior toward the models.
