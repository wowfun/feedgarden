Pocketty is an SSH terminal for iPhone and iPad built around herdr, a host-side tool that keeps coding-agent panes alive and reports whether each pane is working, needs attention, or is done. The author built it during paternity leave so he could stay productive while nap-trapped away from his desk, and reports using it for a couple of months.

How the pieces fit together:

- The app connects directly to your computer over plain SSH. Any reachable SSH host works; Tailscale is suggested as an easy way to reach it from anywhere.
- A small Rust daemon on the host watches herdr. When a pane needs you, it seals the alert to the phone's key with HPKE &#40;X25519, ChaCha20-Poly1305&#41;. A stateless relay on Cloudflare Workers forwards the sealed bytes to Apple Push Notification servers, and a notification extension opens them on the phone. The relay cannot read the contents and stores nothing.
- The SSH key is generated in the Secure Enclave and cannot be exported.
- The terminal uses libghostty-vt for state and draws with wgpu on Metal, so full-screen TUIs render much as they do on a desktop, though on a narrower screen.
- Agent-turn diffs, a file browser, and previews of localhost dev servers started by the agent all run over the same SSH connection, with no port forwarding to set up.

herdr and the daemon are optional; without them the app behaves as a normal SSH client. There is no account to create and the app contains no analytics or tracking. It runs a 14-day free trial with full feature access, then costs a one-time purchase of &#36;99 during a two-week launch promotion and &#36;129 afterwards.
