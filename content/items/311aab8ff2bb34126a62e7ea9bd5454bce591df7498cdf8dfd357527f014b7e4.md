A filmmaker making an animated fantasy feature called Magehold says Claude is currently the model driving the direction, chosen over other models as they advance. In that role it handles all the blocking out of shots and splits them into groups of 30 seconds or less, in a format set up specifically to work with Seedance 2.5. It also generates top-down schematic views that help with spatial awareness and help the video model understand the shots. There is usually still significant back and forth after the first pass: the model produces its take first, the filmmaker checks where it works, then edits the chunks and scenes to fix problems, a process described as faster rather than cheapest.

Consistency work rests on environment plates with no people in them, plus character sheets and prop reference sheets. Now and then, when positioning is critical, the filmmaker takes screen grabs while editing and feeds an earlier shot setup back in as reference. One example given involves keeping objects in the right places on a table, and characters standing under a window, which caused repeated problems until a seating reference from an earlier shot was used to stop the model getting it wrong.

The work is posted on YouTube as it progresses, with the goal of finishing a full 150-minute fantasy feature based on an unreleased book the author wrote. An earlier 80-minute vertical version was made just before this project, so work continues to catch up while using a better video model and tightening parts. The author offers to answer questions about the process and describes the main requirement as telling the model what type of movie is wanted and getting output in a format that is easy to feed to video generators.
