The author observes that "micro apps" — small tools aimed at one specific problem, often built with AI coding tools — are being discussed more often, and asks how the much lower cost of building software changes what is worth shipping.

The central question is sequencing: if an idea can go from concept to working app in a day, does it make more sense to build a small version and check whether anyone actually wants it, rather than spending weeks or months validating first? The author also asks whether the outcome is simply thousands of AI-generated apps that technically work but solve problems nobody cares about.

A second theme is scope. The author wants to know where the line falls between a useful standalone micro app and something that should instead be a feature inside an existing product.

For people already building with AI, the author asks two direct questions: has the lower cost of development changed what you are willing to build, and do you consider "build a lot of small things and see what sticks" a legitimate strategy? The post is a discussion prompt and offers no data or conclusions of its own.
