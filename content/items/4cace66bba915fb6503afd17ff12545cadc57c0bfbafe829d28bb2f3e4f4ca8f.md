FOREST is an open-source framework for autonomous scientific research. Its goal is a research agent that takes a scientific question and works through the research process: exploring ideas, reviewing literature, forming hypotheses, running real experiments, evaluating results, and eventually producing an evidence-grounded research paper.

Instead of relying entirely on a long conversation history, FOREST uses a persistent research graph to track hypotheses, experiments, decisions, failures, and evidence. A major focus is making long-running research agents more reliable by preserving context, maintaining consistent state, recovering from failures, and ensuring scientific claims are actually supported by experimental evidence. The project is early and under active development.

The maintainer explicitly welcomes AI coding agents and autonomous bots as contributors, naming Codex, Claude Code, OpenHands, Factory Droid, and OpenCode. Contributors are not limited to existing issues and can:
- Explore the repository and independently discover bugs or incomplete workflows.
- Open well-researched issues with reproducible evidence.
- Fix existing problems and submit pull requests.
- Improve agent orchestration, state management, persistence, and recovery.
- Work on the frontend, backend, experiment execution, or scientific evidence pipeline.

There is no blanket restriction on AI-generated or AI-assisted contributions, and ordinary well-scoped bug fixes need no special permission. Contributions are judged on correctness, meaningful tests, and engineering quality rather than the contributor's nature. Automated contributions should be verified, avoid duplicates, and follow the contribution guidelines, and larger architectural changes are best discussed first.
