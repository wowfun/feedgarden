A Reddit user &#40;/u/fom&#95;info&#41; describes spending a day at a Google Gemini workshop at NASA Ames while, in parallel, 53 Claude Code sessions ran on their own. The sessions are said to have blocked each other, produced hook messages in capitals, and built, merged and made a tool public before lunch.

The post says the two timelines were cut together into a film premiering at 11:11 PT the same day, with Claude doing the editing: every edit, the cut list and the renderer. Session text, hook messages and commits appear on screen verbatim with their real timestamps, and the piece is described as 220 sessions — 22 minutes, with a teaser shown.

The cut list and scripts are to be released open source at the 220labs GitHub repository as they are finished. The author offers to discuss the setup later but gives no technical detail in the post about how the concurrent sessions were coordinated, and no independent verification of the claims.
