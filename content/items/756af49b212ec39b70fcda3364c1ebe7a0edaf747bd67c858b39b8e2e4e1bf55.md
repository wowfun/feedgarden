The poster argues that the most valuable thing a good assistant does is not always complying, but occasionally telling you your plan has a hole in it before you find out the hard way. They explicitly distinguish this from safety refusals: the cases they mean are ones where they asked for something and the assistant pushed back saying it would cause a problem later.

Their example: they wanted a quick hacky fix, Claude flagged exactly why it would cause trouble within a week, they took the hacky route anyway because they were in a rush, and it caused a problem in exactly six days. The poster asks whether others have a similar "it was annoyingly right" story.

They state they are not looking for complaint threads about over-caution, but genuinely want cases where being told no actually paid off. No additional data or systematic evidence is offered; the post is a request for shared experiences.
