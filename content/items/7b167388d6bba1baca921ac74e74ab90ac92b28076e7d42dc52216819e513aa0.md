A developer with about 25 years of game experience, mostly in AAA and now in indie work, tested how far Claude could take a real game in a few days by building a mashup of 1991's Road Rash and a rally game like DiRT. Opus 5.5 did most of the heavy lifting, producing four vehicles, the physics, drift boost, jump pads and rockets that replace Road Rash's chains.

The hardest part was getting the model to understand how tires and shocks should move against the ground while keeping the game fun and arcade-like. The author says it kept pulling toward realism, which is undesirable when hitting a jump pad at full speed. For visuals they used Astra 6, giving it a screenshot of a benchmark visual and asking it to reach at least 90% of that quality; it ran 3 to 4 hours on most of the 5 maps. Levels were then tweaked by hand in the developer's editor. Music comes from Sandscape and ElevenLabs, and Sandscape handles multiplayer and leaderboards.

The dirt bike, which the author expected to be the most fun, ended up the jankiest element and is still being iterated on. The result builds on roughly three days of AI-assisted work rather than fully autonomous development, since the vehicles and physics were done by the model but level tuning and iteration remained manual.
