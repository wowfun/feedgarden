A Reddit user with over a decade of experience, including staff engineer roles and a master's degree from Georgia Tech, describes struggling to keep an AI coding assistant on target once codebases become decently complex. He says that when a project falls outside the model's training data, it becomes very hard to manage.

His project is a factory that builds agents to work on "business stuff" rather than product engineering. He points to the amount of steering required, the number of entry points, and the volume of circular dependencies as the main difficulties.

He mentions using approaches like expressing more intent and fewer specifics, adding fewer guardrails but more adversarial reviews. He invites others to call it a skill issue and says he is open to being taught how to do better.
