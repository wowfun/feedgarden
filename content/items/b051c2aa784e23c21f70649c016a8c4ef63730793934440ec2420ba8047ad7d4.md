A user complains that Google's Gemini image generator applies stricter, less transparent restrictions to depictions of the female body than to male subjects, and asks whether others have seen the same pattern. They say they have no objection to companies restricting explicit content, but question what they describe as a double standard.

The poster argues that the presence of a woman's body is sometimes treated as inherently inappropriate even when the request is not sexual at all. They point to art, anatomy, historical scenes and ordinary depictions of people as categories that should not automatically become problematic simply because the subject is female.

The complaint also focuses on transparency: users often cannot tell what triggered a restriction, and it is hard to determine whether an identical prompt with a male subject would be treated differently. The author says they are not asking for Gemini to have no restrictions, but for consistency, so that the goal of preventing inappropriate content is pursued with the same standards regardless of gender.

The post ends by inviting concrete examples, especially cases in which similar prompts involving male and female subjects produce different results. No specific prompts, screenshots or reproduced examples are included in the item; the claims rest on the author's impressions.
