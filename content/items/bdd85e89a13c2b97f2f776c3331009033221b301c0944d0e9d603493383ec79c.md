A mechanical engineer with nearly a decade of experience at a small OEM and fabrication shop describes the equipment they design, build and program, from small R&amp;D test stands to large oil and gas pump skids. After a month of using AI at a boss's urging, mainly on documents, they say tasks that used to take an hour or a full day now take minutes to a couple of hours, and they feel more able to multitask. They ask fellow mechanical engineers how they actually use Claude.

Specific questions include whether to use cowork or code, since their work is not app development or true software engineering, and how Claude helps across their tasks: documents, ordering and procurement, schematics such as P&amp;IDs and electrical drawings in nanoCAD 5 or LibreCAD, 3D modeling in SolidWorks, PLC programming mainly in Studio 5000 with some Siemens, LabVIEW for HMIs, and recently OpenFOAM for CFD. They also ask whether code is sufficient for everything from simple research and problem solving to drafting and programming, and which skills, connectors or MCPs people use, hoping to avoid going in blind.
