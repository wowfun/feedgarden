A poster who says they are not an ML specialist asks how Anthropic develops its model line, framing the recent releases as Fable 5.1 first, then Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, and guessing Fable 5.5 may follow soon.

They want to know which of two pictures is closer to reality: that the company concentrates most effort on one flagship model, converts a breakthrough or strong reinforcement-learning result into a state-of-the-art model, and then "nerfs" or distills it into smaller variants for different use cases, performance levels and price points &#40;Opus for expert work, Sonnet for general use, Haiku for speed&#41;; or that each model is essentially its own project with a different architecture, a separate research team and different goals.

They invite employees or people in the field to explain the behind-the-scenes process, and note that when they asked Claude about it, the answer they got was "I don't have inside knowledge of how I was trained." The post closes with a joking aside asking whether "Fable" is itself a nerfed version of "Mythos." No insider answer is provided in the item; the author is /u/zhssnym.
