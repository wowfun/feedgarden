A discussion post revisits "BABA is AI", a 2024 paper presented at that year's ICML &#40;held in Austria&#41;. The paper tested three state-of-the-art multimodal large language models — GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro and Gemini-1.5-Flash — and found, in the sentence the poster highlights, that they fail dramatically when generalization requires the rules of the game to be manipulated and combined. The authors include several MIT researchers and one from Virginia Tech; the post points to the paper PDF and a GitHub repository.

The author contrasts that result with the field as they describe it in late 2026: agentic swarms powered by tera-parameter-class models that, in their framing, can ace ARC-AGI-3 and FrontierMath tier 4 and go on to commit online crimes. They argue that if such systems can still be defeated by the paper's small key-and-door puzzles, the 2024 result has become more important rather than less, and should be relayed to François Chollet and the ARC Foundation as a candidate benchmark for ARC-AGI-4. The author adds that they personally expect agentic swarms to solve these puzzles.

Note: the characterizations of 2026 model capabilities are the poster's assertions, not findings from the paper.
