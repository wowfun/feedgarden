A summary of an Assetnote co-founder's look at how frontier AI models have changed offensive security. According to the account, Claude Opus 4.6–4.8 let researchers increase their output tenfold, but only with careful steering. A Harvard physicist is said to have rated Claude about as capable as a second-year graduate student. GPT 5.6 Sol, described as released in July 2026, went further by finding complex critical bugs with little supervision.

One cited case is wp2shell, a pre-authentication remote code execution flaw in WordPress Core, which the model reportedly found after 6–10 hours of working alone from a prompt adapted from OpenAI's math work. The team now hunts what it calls "internet melting bugs" before attackers find them.

The author expects years of scary vulnerabilities, reasoning that old software sticks around and bug classes rarely disappear. The piece holds out the possibility that new software built with AI may still turn out to be far more secure. Note that the model names, version numbers and dates in this account are unusual and are reported as claims from the linked discussion rather than verified facts.
