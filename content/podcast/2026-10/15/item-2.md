In this episode of The MAD Podcast, host Matt Turck interviews Diogo Almeida, who co-invented RLHF at OpenAI and now leads TypeSafe as CEO. The subject is Jev, which TypeSafe describes as a new kind of "decision model" rather than a text generator. According to the episode, Jev makes fast, cheap and calibrated decisions inside software, and within weeks of launch it became widely copied, with OpenAI, Cloudflare, Amazon and Databricks all shipping their own versions. TypeSafe reportedly raised an &#36;870 million Series A at a &#36;7.5 billion valuation.

Almeida's central argument is a distinction between "System One" and "System Two" behaviour. Reasoning-heavy chat models are framed as unsuited to automation because they are unreliable at a task level, summarized by his phrase "95% Einstein, 5% Mr. Bean." He traces the problem to how models were trained: RLHF taught models to please humans, which he says introduced sycophancy and refusals, while RLVR improved benchmark scores without producing dependable automation. Jev instead uses RLCD, described as reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions, optimizing for what he calls machine-native intelligence.

Practical details Almeida covers include using probabilities and adjustable thresholds so developers retain control, and building Jev on top of open-weight models with training data produced in-house. He argues that "just a classifier" is a compliment, that classification is AI-complete, and that pre-training amounts to wasting resources. He also explains the Jevons paradox business model behind the product name, says he is skeptical of recursive self-improvement, and suggests practical automation may avert the AI winter he once feared. He additionally criticizes benchmarks as useless and discusses the open-weights versus API tradeoff.
