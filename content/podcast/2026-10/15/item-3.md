OpenAI President and co-founder Greg Brockman joins No Priors hosts Sarah Guo and Elad Gil to discuss the company's progress across frontier models, cybersecurity and scientific discovery. In the episode, Brockman says nearly 400 century-old math problems have been solved using OpenAI's AI agents, and argues the technology can similarly accelerate work in biology.

On scientific research, Brockman points to biology's data bottlenecks as a target, and discusses how AI could reshape drug discovery. He also touches on changing regulations as a way to accelerate progress in these areas.

On agents, the conversation covers proactive, always-on agents such as Dots and their effect on modern productivity, the idea of unifying work and consumer agents, and how user interfaces may evolve. Brockman argues that the best UI is no UI at all.

On security, Brockman describes cybersecurity as sitting in a critical window of time in which peak intelligence should be used to find and fix vulnerabilities in systems, and he discusses a shift in security investment. He closes by explaining why OpenAI remains committed to keeping humans in the driver's seat even as models surpass human capability.

The episode's chapters move from AI and mathematics, to AI communication and oversight, biology, drug discovery, regulation, the Dots agent, UI evolution, cybersecurity, security investment and OpenAI's human-centric ethos.
