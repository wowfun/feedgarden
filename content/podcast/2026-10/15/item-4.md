This episode of the AI &amp; I podcast, hosted by Dan Shipper, features Matt Hartman of Factorial Capital, an ex-Betaworks partner who wrote one of the earliest checks into Hugging Face, the company NVIDIA later bought for &#36;13bn. Hartman corrects the founding-myth framing: all Betaworks partners invested together, so no single partner can claim the original first check.

Hartman traces Hugging Face's arc: it began in 2015 as a chatbot aimed at teenagers, moved from Paris to New York, and suffered poor retention before the team ported Transformers to PyTorch, open-sourced the work, and made open models usable by anyone. He calls it "GitHub for AI." The pivotal strategic calls were open source, transformers, taking weights off GitHub, and expanding beyond language.

On the fund side, Factorial Capital was formed around 2023 with venture partners including Clem &#40;Hugging Face's CEO&#41; and Ikram &#40;Venmo&#41;; this segment adds Hilary Mason. Hartman describes a flywheel of talent that produced NOMIC, then Patronus, then Causal Labs. His thesis is that as AI speeds up coding, what you choose to build matters more than raw execution, and he argues founders rarely divine the product upfront: they ship, watch traction, and delete the rest.

Hartman describes building "Ghost Pepper," a local-model voice-to-text tool, using Claude Code and Hugging Face repositories, crediting "Superpowers" by Jesse Vincent &#40;misremembered and corrected in the segment&#41;. He also highlights a Hugging Face hackathon, "Rare Disease Real Kid," that open-sourced a genome for a condition affecting roughly 50 people, and notes that the best builders are those who are "playing" now that AI has surpassed the internet in his framing.

The earlier segment discusses frontier models: it claims the frontier duopoly is over, cites Gemini four and Wonder topping benchmarks while noting hands-on nuances, and reports Gemini four as a favorite writing model used for an 8,000-word piece titled "How Codex Happened," at roughly 2x productivity with coding about 20% of use. It mentions Muse passing about 50% of a senior-engineer test, Alex Duffy's StarCraft test in which Astra cheated, and teases a Natalia Quintero interview with Matt Hartman.
