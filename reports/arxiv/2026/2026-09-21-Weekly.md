## 1. Business Compromise Detection with Agentic AI and LLM-driven Knowledge Discovery

The authors report that an LLM agent is a strong but precision-poor final arbiter for detecting compromised ad accounts, so they keep it as an investigator emitting structured signals and delegate verdicts to a neuro-symbolic stage &#40;FOIL-IE rules, Naive Bayes calibration, a contradiction layer&#41;. This raised MCC from 0.295 to 0.435, lifting precision from 0.250 to 0.446 at a recall cost &#40;0.920 to 0.660&#41;, and edged tree ensembles &#40;0.386&#41;.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32643v1) · 2026-09-26

## 2. IChart2Code: Benchmarking Multimodal Large Language Models for Interactive Chart Code Generation

IChart2Code is a benchmark of 377 tasks across 20 chart forms and 13 data families with 1209 interaction requirements, targeting executable HTML/JavaScript interactive chart code, paired with a browser-based evaluation protocol &#40;Executability gate plus Data Fidelity, Static Visual Correctness and Interaction Correctness&#41;. The authors also propose TRAIL, a dual-agent framework; across four MLLMs it improved those four dimensions by 7.89, 4.98, 3.45 and 4.47 percentage points over direct prompting.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32413v1) · 2026-09-26

## 3. Toward Agentic Optical Networks: A Vision of LLM Agent-Driven Autonomous Lifecycle Management

This vision paper argues that LLM agents can extend optical network automation beyond traditional AI, while noting their use is still early and limited by weak multi-task coordination, high compute demands, data dependence and reliability concerns. Its core contribution is a proposed hierarchical multi-agent framework covering all lifecycle management phases, from planning and deployment to maintenance, upgrade and decommission.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32226v1) · 2026-09-26

## 4. Who Is Behind the Harness? Fingerprinting LLMs through Agentic Behavior

The authors present LIDAR, an active black-box method that fingerprints coding-agent execution behavior rather than text or token distributions, using three coding probe pairs to expose post-edit verification, transient-failure recovery and specification-test conflict resolution. Across 36 models from seven families and two agent harnesses, they report high Top-1 accuracy and MRR, outperforming four existing fingerprinting and API-auditing baselines, with no access to weights, logits or provider internals.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28559v1) · 2026-09-23

## 5. AgenticSizing: A Large Language Model-based Multi-Agent Framework for Analog Circuit Sizing

This paper proposes a multi-agent LLM framework for analog circuit sizing that analyzes circuit topology, decomposes netlists into functional blocks and substructures, extracts reusable lightweight design knowledge, and has a planner coordinate role-specialized sizing agents. Validated on eight circuits &#40;largest with up to 55 transistors and 60 sizing variables&#41;, it reportedly achieved a 60% success rate on the LDO benchmark in an average of 83 iterations, where classical optimizers found no feasible solutions; source code is available.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25873v1) · 2026-09-22

## 6. When Do Agents Help? Embedding, LLM and Agentic Alignment of Classical Texts and Their Translations

Comparing seven systems on 452 classical texts &#40;9,833 human-aligned units&#41;, the author reports generative workflows recovered 93-94% of references versus at most 77% for embeddings. The agent's gain over a direct LLM call was 0.5 percentage points &#40;95% CI -0.02 to 1.17&#41; and auditing added no established benefit; agents gave structurally valid output for all 452 texts.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33691v1) · 2026-09-27 · By Máté Metzger

## 7. LLM4Trust: Exploring the Capabilities of Large Language Models for Trust Evaluation

LLM4Trust is proposed as a benchmark framework for exploring LLMs in trust evaluation: it builds trust graphs modeling five basic trust properties, tests eight LLMs under nine prompt methods, and applies top combinations to five real-world datasets. Experiments suggest LLMs can understand these properties and show real-world potential under limited supervision, but stay vulnerable to attacks on trust graphs and few-shot demonstrations and incur high inference costs.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33521v1) · 2026-09-27

## 8. Can Open-Weight Large Language Models &#40;LLMs&#41; Simulate Human Survey Populations? A Cross-Instrument Calibration Study

This study evaluates three open-weight LLM families on cross-instrument calibration, conditioning personas on real respondents' verbatim answers to one psychometric instrument and measuring them on a second, checked against a 2,058-person human panel. Across a 139-pair grid the simulated cross-instrument correlation tracked the real human correlation at r = 0.70-0.73 in every model; the authors note the capability does not improve monotonically across releases.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32638v1) · 2026-09-26

## 9. DeepEdu-v1: Efficient and Scalable Agentic LLMs for Vietnamese Education

DeepEdu-v1 is an AI tutoring system for Vietnamese education built on SCALE, an engine that pairs a long-context inference engine amortizing token selection at cluster granularity with a self-improving agentic layer curating a verified playbook instead of fine-tuning. The authors report 7.7x fewer retrieval calls than a selective-attention baseline and about 35% lower prefill latency, and in deployment a nearly 2x TTFT speedup over standard vLLM serving with agentic accuracy rising from 70.0% to 79.5% on complex tasks.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31568v1) · 2026-09-25

## 10. DynBranch: Speculative Subgraph Reuse for Dynamic Agentic LLM Serving

DynBranch targets the branch-resolution barrier in agentic LLM workflows, where downstream computation cannot start until a runtime branch resolves and caching cannot hide it because the reuse key is unknown. It makes an unresolved branch addressable via a stable coordinate so candidate subgraphs run during resolution and completed results are reused across requests, via a two-level controller at the model-API boundary. The authors report up to 32% lower mean latency than each workload's strongest prior system on Qwen3-32B with 4x H200 GPUs.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31047v1) · 2026-09-25

## 11. Coding Agents Aren't Enough&#33; Evaluating an Enterprise Security Brain for Agentic Cloud Investigations

The authors evaluate the Sola Security Brain, whose relational substrate is resolved offline with security logic applied at query time, against Claude Code and OpenAI Codex operating the same live AWS environment via a read-only CLI on 28 investigation tasks. They report the Brain reaching 0.549 +/- 0.012 coverage versus 0.340 +/- 0.011 and 0.281 +/- 0.006, leading 24 of 28 tasks at 17.8x and 20.8x lower cost per task, and describe a "sample-and-generalise" answer pattern in which agents enumerate part of a population yet assert an unhedged universal negative.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30345v2) · 2026-09-24

## 12. On the Impact of Requirement Smells in LLM-Based Code Generation

Building on a prior traceability study, the authors reuse its dataset and requirement smell taxonomy, extending it with system tests for four applications. They report that increasing smell density was generally associated with lower test-suite-based functional correctness, that smell categories had similar effects, and that non-smelly requirements could still yield faulty code; they suggest code generation is more sensitive to smells than traceability.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29208v1) · 2026-09-24

## 13. Unity Insight: A Production Code--Asset Index for LLM Coding Agents in Unity Projects

The authors present Unity Insight, described as a persistent, LLM-facing, agent-integrated cross-file code-asset index for Unity projects, shipping in production with the Tuanjie Codely agent CLI since its 2026-07-28 public launch. In a paired experiment of 28 project-specific questions on two Unity games with the same model and harness, they report the index-backed agent used 53% fewer tokens and 52% less wall-clock time than a general-purpose exploration agent &#40;exact paired sign tests, p&lt;0.004&#41;.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27585v1) · 2026-09-23

## 14. KITE: KV-Invariant Transformer Expansion for Efficient Agentic LLM Scaling

The authors introduce KV-Invariant Transformer Expansion &#40;KITE&#41;, a scaling paradigm that trains a model up from a smaller to a larger size while placing added parameters in regions that do not affect attention KV, so prefilling KV relies only on the smaller part of the model. As an instantiation they present Step Scale Transformer &#40;SST&#41;, reported to achieve lower training loss than 47B and 63B MoE Transformers at comparable cumulative training compute while reducing estimated inference cost by 6.7% and 31.6%.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27294v1) · 2026-09-23

## 15. Crossflow: Prefill-Decode Elasticity for Agentic LLM Serving

Noting that prefill-decode disaggregation rests on a static partition while phase demand varies sharply, the authors present Crossflow, which makes the boundary elastic without changing node roles: each decode node publishes a short-lived, revocable lease bounding local-prefill compute, KV capacity, transfer work, and projected output. They report 16.2-17.4% geometric-mean token throughput gains over static P/D and up to 43.4% at high load, with lower mean TTFT at every evaluated point.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27085v1) · 2026-09-22

## 16. AgentBoundary: Counterfactual Evaluation of Safety in Tool-Using LLM Agents

The authors introduce AgentBound, described as a four-way counterfactual generation-and-evaluation framework for tool-using agent safety that independently varies apparent risk and action permissibility, instantiated as a human-validated 4,000-task suite. Across 17 model and harness configurations they report high safety often co-occurring with poor authorized-task completion &#40;GPT-5.5 blocks 99.5% of routine-looking unauthorized actions yet completes 28.7% of risky-looking authorized tasks&#41;, and a runtime calibration module improving authorized completion by 18.2%.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33658v1) · 2026-09-27

## 17. Trajectory Unlearning on LLM-based Agents

The authors introduce trajectory-level unlearning, formulating removal of specific action trajectories in long-horizon agentic tasks rather than factual knowledge, and noting that trajectories are sequentially dependent and cannot be decomposed into isolated prompt-response pairs. They propose Group-injected Relative Policy Optimization &#40;GiRPO&#41; and report that on ALFWorld and WebShop it unlearns target trajectories while preserving task success rates, outperforming knowledge-unlearning baselines on forgetting quality and task utility.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33639v1) · 2026-09-27

## 18. API Secrets Should Never Become Tokens in the LLM's Vocabulary: A Threat Analysis of API Credential Handling in LLM Agent Systems and an Empirical Evaluation of a Vault-Mediated Execution Boundary

The authors formalize a credential-exposure threat chain for agentic systems and describe a vault-mediated architecture in which the model selects a connector identifier while a trusted request boundary supplies authentication. Evaluating a production implementation, Corvic Security Vault, across 16 probes spanning seven control domains, they report every probe met its expected outcome, and also report a negative result: a connector whose stored header mapping failed its provider's authentication contract.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33371v1) · 2026-09-27

## 19. Agentic Multi-Turn Reasoning: A Fairness Approach

The authors identify long-horizon credit assignment and imbalanced data distributions as key difficulties in learning agentic systems, and propose Fair Multi-Level Preference Optimization &#40;Fair-MPO or Phi-MPO&#41;, a preference optimization framework that adds a Fair Multi-Level Objective to address imbalance. They report theoretical analysis of both issues and state-of-the-art performance on agentic reasoning benchmarks.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33323v1) · 2026-09-27

## 20. Robust Hierarchical Structures for Agentic Document Analysis

The authors target a robust and compact inferred document structure, where robustness means each subsection's text is a superset of the true structure's text under the same header and compactness minimizes that superset. They propose SHED, a two-stage workflow with a pluggable first stage, reporting F-1 improvements of 13%-68% over non-LLM baselines and 9%-15% over LLM-based approaches, and that agents using SHED-inferred structures gain 3%-23% accuracy at up to 10x lower cost.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33322v1) · 2026-09-27

## 21. On Device Agentic Operation Caches -- Classifier-Centric NL-to-Action Generation

The paper proposes converting the natural-language-to-action problem from a generative task into a classification-centric formulation using on-device operation caches, so frequent action classes can be handled locally. For an Excel formula generation task, the authors report a 56% reduction in total inference cost versus cloud-only model-routing inference and a 5x reduction in latency to response latency on cache hits.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33141v1) · 2026-09-27

## 22. MedRouter: Demystifying Knowledge Differences Across Medical LLMs for Routing-Based Reasoning

MedRouter is an agentic system that uses an embedding-based multi-label router to select and query specialist medical LLMs, then passes their responses to a generator. Its SCALE two-stage training framework first trains the router with specialist correctness supervision, then optimizes selections via reinforcement learning with a Performance Gain Reward. On eight medical QA benchmarks, the authors report average accuracy 8% above the strongest routing baseline.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33119v1) · 2026-09-27

## 23. Theory of Scene: Breaking the Symmetry Trap in Multi-Agent LLM Coordination

The authors describe a symmetry trap: homogeneous LLM agents acting concurrently without communication collide on targets they must split and diverge on targets they must take together, and Theory of Mind cannot escape it. They propose Theory of Scene &#40;ToS&#41;, a training-free schema where each agent reads its public role and the shared task context via role gating and task coupling. ToS reportedly beats six baselines, raising DivvyBench success from 71.1% to 99.6%.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32939v1) · 2026-09-26

## 24. ARSM: Auto-Regressive State Machine for Agentic Reasoning Compression

ARSM is described as a lightweight, training-free framework for in-situ reasoning compression through structured state evolution. It combines a trajectory abstraction mechanism that reorganizes interaction histories into compact Hypothesis-Action-Result micro-chains with a dynamic state machine regulating hierarchical memory. Evaluated on Webshop, Multi-Objective Multi-Hop QA and SWE-Bench Lite, the authors report maintained task performance with reduced token consumption.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32852v1) · 2026-09-26

## 25. Fail Loudly: An Auditable Runtime for Agentic Data Analysis

RADAR is presented as an auditable runtime that makes an agent's analytical choices inspectable and supports revision through execution feedback, addressing silent errors in agentic data analysis. It uses an evidence-preserving exploration module, typed operators that record declared inputs, operation arguments and observations, and runtime validation that rejects operations or returns diagnostic feedback on conflict. The authors report KramaBench scores of 0.723 with full source retrieval and 0.747 with gold sources, and relative gains of 14.0% on DA-Code and 59.3% on DABStep.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32528v1) · 2026-09-26

## 26. Beyond Scripted Search: Sample-Efficient Reward Discovery via Agentic Black-box Optimization

The authors propose Agentic Reward Black-box Optimization &#40;ARBO&#41;, in which an LLM agent builds the reward-function search strategy at run time from an evaluation history kept as its persistent workspace, containing oracle-maintained observations &#40;candidate scores, per-term training curves, error tracebacks&#41; and the agent's own recorded beliefs and next steps. Across four control domains, the authors report gains of 29.9% in manipulation success rate and 192.8% in power-grid score over baseline means under a shared evaluation budget, with ablations on components and backbone choice.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32394v1) · 2026-09-26

## 27. Solving Every Step Is Not Enough: Milestone Oracles Reveal a Composition Gap in LLM Math Reasoning

The authors introduce OracleLadder, a diagnostic evaluation that supplies models increasing levels of oracle help to locate where math reasoning fails: no help, a teacher-written roadmap of milestones, roadmap plus milestone answers, and each milestone alone. On 354 NuminaMath problems and six models from 8B to 671B parameters, the largest gap for every model is reported as the composition gap, covering 33-48% of problems, or 24-37% after removing problems flagged as grading errors.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32235v1) · 2026-09-26

## 28. OptiArena: Can LLMs Improve Executable Algorithms under Fixed Resource Budgets?

OptiArena is introduced as a budget-controlled testbed for studying whether LLMs can improve executable game-playing algorithms through five rounds of code edits within a fixed minimal scaffold, bounded evaluator feedback and fixed resource budgets, with LLM API cost reported separately from local evaluator wall-clock. Across twelve frontier LLMs and five games, the authors report that models improve designated weak starters more consistently than they refine editable competent baselines, with substantial variation across games and models.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32227v1) · 2026-09-26

## 29. Overview of the TREC 2025 Million Large Language Models track

The track operationalizes a retrieval-based paradigm for expert selection in agentic AI, shifting the retrieval target from documents to expert LLMs, rather than relying on predefined capability statements. Participants receive a discovery set of queries, answers and log-probabilities from more than one thousand LLMs and must infer expertise representations and rank LLMs by expected performance on unseen test queries. The authors describe it as the first large-scale benchmark for expertise retrieval in agentic AI.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31921v1) · 2026-09-25

## 30. Improving Medical Calculation of LLMs with Embedded Coding

MedCode is a framework that trains LLMs to generate embedded executable code for medical calculation: given a clinical context, the model identifies the relevant calculator, extracts its input variables, and produces a script that delegates arithmetic to a deterministic interpreter, returning the value with an explanation and unit. The authors build SFT and preference datasets from MedCalc and curate an ICU calculation dataset, and propose weighted Direct Preference Optimization &#40;wDPO&#41;. With LLaMA3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B they report absolute accuracy gains of 20-30 percentage points.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31908v1) · 2026-09-25

## 31. Structured Reasoning Agentic Framework for Interpretable Critical View of Safety Assessment

The authors report 68.1% mAP on the Endoscapes-CVS201 benchmark with criterion-level explanations.

## 34. Cheap, open agents make LLM pollution harder to mitigate

The authors compared nine agent configurations, from fully open to closed commercial, that autonomously completed a survey with multiple response types and various detection checks. They report that fully open agents ran locally without usage fees and performed competitively with commercial alternatives, that open and commercial agents failed different checks with no single check reliably detecting all agents, and that open-text responses discriminated best between agents and humans.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31054v1) · 2026-09-25

## 35. MoMHa: Multi-Objective Optimization of LLM Harnesses over Accuracy, Safety, and Tokens

The authors present Meta-Harness, which frames harness design as search over accuracy, behavioural safety and token cost using an agentic proposer with access to prior harness source, traces and scoring artifacts. They report that a single-phase joint-reward proposer &#40;MoMHa&#41; outperformed alternatives across seventeen domains, scoring jointly 0.482 versus 0.198–0.422 for ten baselines on synthetic suites and 0.461 versus 0.377 for the strongest baseline &#40;DSPy&#41; on real-world benchmarks, with transfer to unseen benchmarks on 8 of 12 target models.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30967v1) · 2026-09-25

## 36. ORCA: Evaluating LLMs on Data Science Code Translation

The authors introduce ORCA, a benchmark for data science code translation &#40;DSCT&#41; with ORCA-MAIN &#40;1,600 grounding-level tasks across data querying, manipulation and deep learning&#41; and ORCA-PROJECT &#40;200 tasks over complete projects in 7 task types&#41;, each with reference translations and functional-equivalence test cases. They report Claude-Opus-4.6 reaching 56.92% success on ORCA-MAIN and 33.67% on ORCA-PROJECT, note that more explicit fine-grained source operations ease translation, and propose an intent-augmented method gaining 4.80% and 5.33% absolute on the two sets.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30749v1) · 2026-09-25

## 37. GRASP: Generating, Revising, and Assessing for Strategic Planning with Agentic AI

The authors introduce GRASP, a strategy-aware multi-stage planning framework that pre-compiles global macro-guidelines, explores alternative localized strategies in isolated context windows, and independently evaluates trajectories with a multi-criteria discriminator. They report accuracy gains over direct LLM planners on Natural Plan Calendar Scheduling &#40;&#126;12.4%&#41;, ZebraLogic &#40;&#126;30.8%&#41; and SciBench Math, elimination of the multi-task degradation penalty, up to 16.7% absolute gain in interleaved dual-task settings, and a 14.5% margin over frontier reasoning models such as GPT-5-mini.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30147v1) · 2026-09-24

## 38. agentic-ger: terminology recovery in long-form speech using global context

The authors propose Agentic-GER, an LLM-based agent for terminology correction in long-form speech that uses global context from the full transcript to flag suspicious terms, selectively re-transcribes source speech to check candidate corrections, and lets accepted edits guide later decisions. Experiments with four LLMs and two ASR systems on GigaSpeechBench show consistent terminology improvements in Chinese and English, with up to a 36.8% relative reduction in biased character error rate over the Whisper baseline on Chinese speech.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29428v1) · 2026-09-24

## 39. Epistemic-Probabilistic Model for Guarded Multi-Agent LLM Coordination

The authors introduce Epistemic Probabilistic Language Agents &#40;EPLA&#41;, a neuro-symbolic architecture for multi-agent coordination under uncertainty in which a Symbolic Guard gives structured diagnostic feedback, the LLM generates typed actions, and the Guard controls execution against an authoritative symbolic state. They formalize the epistemic layer in a gossip testbed through epistemic lottery gossip models combining view-based call histories with agent-indexed probability weights, arguing such a formalism can address shortcomings of agentic LLMs.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29366v1) · 2026-09-24

## 40. Back to the Definition: Estimating Step-Level Advantages via Trajectory Graphs for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

The authors argue group-based RL methods such as GRPO give systematically biased step-level advantages because coarse trajectory-level advantages poorly reflect individual step contributions. They propose GRAFT, which grafts rollout trajectories into a trajectory graph, recovers node state-values via Bellman iteration and assigns credit by node value differences, plus Graph GAE to reduce state-value estimation bias. They report consistent gains over GRPO on multi-turn agentic benchmarks.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28963v1) · 2026-09-24

## 41. Shopping by algorithm: How agentic AI deploys human heuristics as a surrogate consumer

Using "Tool-Lab," which places product attributes behind costly tool calls, the authors trace pre-choice information acquisition in eight deployed LLMs from three providers. Pricing cues rarely mislead at zero cost, but acquisition costs under a vague goal prompt lead LLMs to omit diagnostic attributes needed to compute unit price and choose suboptimal options resembling human heuristics; the vulnerability is attributed to storefront information architecture, not immutable LLM flaws.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28372v1) · 2026-09-23

## 42. BaseCamp --- An Agentic AI Framework for Automating DNA Sequencing Data Pipelines

BaseCamp automates the decision layer of DNA sequencing pipelines via six specialized AI agents covering sample intake and quality control, alignment, variant calling, annotation, cross-stage monitoring and reporting. Agents do not perform sequence analysis; they select, configure and interpret established tools, with reasoning powered by fine-tuned domain-specialized LLMs under human-in-the-loop orchestration. Evaluation reports configurations concordant with expert practice, an inspectable filtering ledger, and cross-stage anomaly detection.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28557v1) · 2026-09-23

## 43. Recovering Agentic Sovereignty: Mitigating the Consensus Paradox via Contrastive Epistemic Decoding

The authors introduce Contrastive Epistemic Decoding &#40;CED&#41;, a zero-shot inference intervention that uses a dual forward-pass on a single architecture, plus an asymmetric zero-bounded probability clamp and a discrete top-k truncation mask, to suppress conformity bias without grammatical collapse. Evaluated on 7,200 paired trajectories across GAIA, SWE-bench and Multi-Challenge with Gemma-2 &#40;9B&#41;, Llama-3.1 &#40;8B&#41; and Mistral v0.3 &#40;7B&#41;, they report reduced cognitive loafing by up to 33.00% absolute and up to 30.75% accuracy recovery, without fine-tuning.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25570v1) · 2026-09-22

## 44. The AI Neuroscientist: An Interactive Agentic Interface for Neuroimaging Analysis

The AI Neuroscientist is a language agent for interactive neuroimaging data exploration that integrates a large language model with a neuroimaging toolset for quality control, modeling and visualization, letting researchers query data quality and specify analysis parameters in natural language as an alternative to scripted pipelines for small-scale exploration. The authors demonstrate it on fNIRS data and evaluate it on a custom fNIRS benchmark against general-purpose LLM agents with code sandboxes; future work will extend the architecture to modalities such as fMRI.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25254v1) · 2026-09-21

## 45. GRUET: Quantifying Uncertainty of Agentic Reasoning-and-Acting Processes

GRUET is a method for uncertainty quantification of ReAct agents that combines turn-level reasoning uncertainty quantification—modeling the reasoning space spanned by potential reasoning branches as a graph and approximating its complexity with graph complexity—with trajectory-level uncertainty aggregation for overall trajectory credibility. The authors report empirical evaluations across nine LLMs and five benchmarks validating its effectiveness in selective generation performance, measured by AUROC, AUPRC and AUARC.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24831v1) · 2026-09-21

## 46. Agentic Video Understanding: A Survey

This survey reviews video understanding agents: systems that use video as the primary information source and solve understanding tasks through adaptive state construction and action selection. It formalizes an agent loop, organizes the literature via a challenge-to-design taxonomy linking context bottlenecks to hierarchical evidence memory, evidence sparsity to active evidence acquisition, temporal causality to state tracking, and multimodal ambiguity to role-specialized coordination, and reviews paradigms, supervision signals, benchmarks and evaluation protocols.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31713v1) · 2026-09-21

## 47. FLARE: A Full-Lifecycle Dense Supervision Paradigm for Long-Horizon Coding Agents via Generative Reward Model

FLARE &#40;Full-Lifecycle Alignment and Reward Engine&#41; is a dense supervision paradigm driven by a lightweight Generative Reward Model &#40;GRM&#41;, distilled by RADAR, which extracts hindsight-free supervision via causal-chain backtracking. FLARE uses the GRM at inference as an Active Scaffold intercepting high-risk generation steps for localized breakpoint re-execution, and post-training as SFT reranking scores and step-level dense RL rewards. The authors report FLARE &#40;N=1&#41; beating Global Rollout &#40;N=5&#41; with 5x fewer tokens, 19.13% relative SFT gains and 9.19% RL improvement.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23808v1) · 2026-09-20

## 48. One Model Is Not a Crowd: Multi-LLM and Aspect-Conditioned Diverse Comment Generation

The authors hypothesize that comment diversity is better approximated by combining multiple LLMs with aspect-conditioned generation, and introduce a framework characterizing diversity along three axes—dispersion, coverage and alignment—across semantic, linguistic and socio-pragmatic features. In a study of over 2 million YouTube comments across multiple domains, they report that multi-LLM and aspect-conditioned generation better align with human comment distributions and remain viable under pretraining-style curation and effective downstream, though human diversity remains unmatched.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33666v1) · 2026-09-27

## 49. You Only Edit Once: Incentivizing In-Context Capability of LLMs via Local Demonstration Refinement

This work frames in-context learning demonstration selection as a constrained local search problem and presents local demonstration editing &#40;LDE&#41;, which applies a single structured edit to an initially retrieved demonstration set using Keep, Delete or Replace actions. LDE is reduced to a policy search problem for which a small LLM, Jev-LDE, is trained with reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards; at test time it performs one edit followed by one inference from the target LLM. The authors report consistent ICL gains across classification benchmarks and transfer without retraining.

## 50. TGRL: Temperature-Grouped Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Exploration in LLMs

TGRL converts temperature-induced diversity into an explicit training signal: for each prompt it splits the rollout group into low- and high-temperature subsets, estimates exploration gain from their reward contrast, and assigns this group-level signal as token-level credit via Jensen–Shannon divergence between the temperature-scaled next-token distributions. The authors report equivalent accuracy up to 36% faster than strong RLVR baselines without expanding the rollout budget, plus gains across 11 benchmarks.
